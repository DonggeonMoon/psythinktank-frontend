[Today's Stock] Earnings Outlook Up, Shares Down 32% in a Year: What's Going On at Gildan Activewear?

psythinktank_RA|2026.10.31
조회수 00 views閲覧数 0浏览量 0

Earnings Rose, So Why Did the Stock Fall?

Shares of Gildan Activewear (NYSE: GIL), the Canadian maker of basic apparel such as T-shirts, socks and underwear, recently trade around $40. The closing price on October 27 was about $40.06, roughly 31.9% lower than a year earlier. This year the company raised its earnings outlook, yet the stock moved the opposite way. This article focuses on that gap.

Background: The Hanesbrands Acquisition Made It Bigger

Gildan is a vertically integrated manufacturer that makes and sells blank T-shirts (plain tees for printing), socks and underwear. Vertical integration means handling everything from yarn production to sewing in its own factories. It has plants in Central America, the Caribbean and Bangladesh. This year it acquired the U.S. underwear and sock brand Hanesbrands, which changed the scale of the business significantly.

  • Second-quarter 2026 revenue (continuing operations) was about $1.58 billion, up roughly 72% from a year earlier. Most of the increase comes from the addition of Hanesbrands.
  • Adjusted earnings per share (EPS) were $1.28, up about 32% and above the market estimate of about $1.11. Adjusted EPS is per-share profit calculated excluding one-time costs.
  • The company raised its full-year adjusted EPS outlook to $4.65–$4.75. The previous outlook was $4.20–$4.40.
  • It expects full-year revenue to land at the low end of its prior range ($6.0–$6.2 billion).
  • It expects free cash flow of about $1 billion. Free cash flow is cash earned from operations minus capital expenditures.

Dividing the share price by this year's EPS outlook gives a multiple of 8 to 9. This is my own estimate, and keep in mind it is based on adjusted earnings that exclude one-time items. The $1 billion free cash flow outlook is also not small relative to the company's market value. No confirmed market capitalization figure is available. Multiplying the share price by roughly 150 million shares outstanding suggests a figure in the $6 billion range (an estimate).

What the Market May Be Worried About (Estimates)

  • Debt burden: The acquisition added debt. The company said it will sell its Australian Hanesbrands business for about A$700 million (about $490 million) and use the proceeds to repay bank loans. It aims to close the deal in the second half of 2026.
  • Revenue at the low end: The earnings outlook was raised, but revenue sits at the bottom of the range. Concerns about slowing consumer demand or inventory adjustments may be priced in. This is my interpretation, not a confirmed fact.
  • Integration risk: The company expects cost savings (synergies) of $250 million a year over three years and plans to achieve about $100 million this year. Whether factory consolidation proceeds as planned is the key.

Next Event: Q3 Results on November 5

The company has announced it will report third-quarter 2026 results on November 5 (local time). Market research sites show estimates of around $1.60 EPS and about $1.64 billion in revenue, though these may differ by data provider. Points to watch:

  • Whether it maintains or raises the full-year EPS outlook ($4.65–$4.75)
  • Whether the $100 million cost-savings target is on track
  • The pace of net debt reduction and progress on the sale of the Australian business
  • Whether revenue growth holds up even excluding the acquisition effect

Summary

The earnings outlook went up and the share price went down. The market therefore seems to care more about debt, the integration process and consumer conditions than about the earnings figures. The November 5 report should offer some answers. Prices in this article are based on the October 27 close, so check live quotes separately.
