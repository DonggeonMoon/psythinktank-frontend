[Today's Stock] Japanese Stock SHIFT Poured $17M Into AI and Watched Profits Halve, Then Reversed Course in Q3

관리자|2026.09.27
조회수 55 views閲覧数 5浏览量 5

What Kind of Company Is This

SHIFT was founded in 2005 and built its core business around software testing outsourcing. Think of it as a company that companies hire to hunt for bugs and verify quality before a new system or app goes live.

What sets it apart is a strategy called the "One-SHIFT" concept, under which it keeps acquiring companies in adjacent fields to grow its footprint. It has picked up data analytics firms, web marketing agencies, and system development companies, folding them all into one group. More recently it has layered AI-driven test automation services on top, trying to build a new growth engine.

The First Half Wasn't Pretty

Looking at H1 of fiscal year 2026 (September through February, on a September-to-August fiscal calendar), revenue grew nicely, but profit actually fell.

H1 revenue came in at ¥72.035 billion (up 16.8%), H1 operating profit at ¥6.907 billion (down 14.3%), while Q3 alone saw operating profit of ¥4.476 billion (up 16.5% year over year).

The profit decline wasn't due to M&A costs but to AI investment. The company poured roughly ¥2.5 billion into AI development in H1 alone, and hiring also returned to normal levels, pushing costs higher. This wasn't a case of the business struggling, it was a case of spending ahead of the curve for the future.

But the picture shifts when you look at Q3 (March through May): operating profit actually rose 16.5% year over year. That looks like an early signal that the AI investment is starting to pay off through efficiency gains.

What the Company Is Projecting for the Year

Full-year guidance hasn't changed. The company is targeting revenue of ¥150 billion (+15.5%), operating profit of ¥20 billion (+13.4%), and net profit of ¥13.5 billion (+24.3%). Holding onto these numbers despite a rocky first half suggests the company itself is confident about a second-half recovery.

Is the Stock Cheap or Expensive Right Now

The current price sits at ¥881.1. That's already up more than 49% from the 52-week low of ¥590, while it still sits about 37% below the 52-week high of ¥1,410.

Looking at the theoretical fair value calculated from the company's own historical data, the PBR-based range runs from a floor of ¥550 to a fair value of ¥790 up to a ceiling of ¥1,030, with the PER-based range showing a similar pattern. The current price sits just above that fair-value mark. It's hard to call it cheap, but it isn't stretched either, it's sitting in an ambiguous middle zone.

Analyst consensus price targets, meanwhile, average ¥1,119 to ¥1,174, which is 27% to 33% above the current price, with ratings leaning moderately bullish. That suggests the market is already pricing in some expectation of a second-half recovery.

By the company's own math, the stock is near fair value. Analysts think it goes higher than that. Second-half earnings are what will close that gap.

Bottom Line

SHIFT's revenue growth has been consistent. The real question is how reliably profit follows that growth, and in H1 it wobbled because of AI spending before bouncing back in Q3.

  • Positives: Q3 profit rebound, unchanged full-year guidance, expanding AI business line

  • Concerns: the H1 profit slump precedent, the stock sitting near the top of its own theoretical valuation band, and a price already well off its 52-week low

Rather than jumping in right now, it may be worth waiting for one more quarter of results to confirm whether this rebound actually holds.
