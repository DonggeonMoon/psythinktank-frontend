[Today's Stock] Sales Down 17%, Yet Net Profit Turns Positive: The Strange Numbers at COLOPL

psythinktank_RA|2026.10.12
조회수 00 views閲覧数 0浏览量 0

Sales fell, but profit rose

Japanese mobile game developer COLOPL (3668) posted seemingly contradictory figures in its nine-month results for the fiscal year ending September 2026 (October 2025 to June 2026). Sales declined, yet profit rose sharply. This is the first thing to look at when examining this stock right now.

ItemNine-month cumulative
SalesAbout 15.7 billion yen (down about 17% year on year)
Operating profitAbout 1.3 billion yen
Ordinary profitAbout 2.5 billion yen (about 2.4 times the prior year)
Net profitAbout 1.5 billion yen (back in the black from a loss a year earlier)

Looking at the April–June quarter alone, ordinary profit was about 1.09 billion yen in the black, versus a loss of about 950 million yen in the same quarter a year earlier. Ordinary profit is operating profit plus non-operating items such as interest and valuation gains and losses. Because ordinary profit (2.5 billion yen) is much larger than operating profit (1.3 billion yen), you should be aware that items other than the core business are supporting profit considerably. We were unable to confirm the specific breakdown this time.

Share price: closed at 389 yen on October 9, 2026

  • October 12 is a Japanese public holiday (Sports Day), so the market is closed. As of the most recent trading day, October 9, the share price was 389 yen (+8 yen from the previous day).
  • Market capitalization is about 50.7 billion yen.
  • According to PSY Thinktank data, the one-year share price change is about -11.9%. Even though profit has turned around, the stock is lower than it was a year ago.
  • Median annual trading value is about 170 million yen, which means trading is thin for large amounts of money to move in and out.
  • The data provided shows a dividend of 20 yen per share. A simple calculation at 389 yen gives about 5%, but the actual dividend policy needs to be checked separately in the company's disclosures.

What the market is watching: the new-title pipeline

In its earnings briefing materials, the company presented a pipeline of 5 smartphone titles and 10 PC/console titles. It is an attempt to expand from a mobile-centered business into PC and console. At the August earnings announcement, it unveiled a new title, "Archtive Cinema RPG 369," with the release date still undecided. It was also reported that the company said the response to the announcement of "Shiro Neko Project INFINITY" was strong.

A game company's results depend heavily on whether a hit title emerges. That profit improved while sales from existing games declined may be a sign that the cost structure has been made leaner. However, this is my own estimate, and a sales rebound will only be possible once the performance of the new titles is confirmed.

The gap between market forecasts and company guidance

The company does not publish a full-year earnings forecast. The securities consensus (the analysts' average forecast) appears to put ordinary profit for the September fiscal year at about 400 million yen. Given that the company has already posted 2.5 billion yen in the nine-month cumulative period, such a low figure suggests the data timing or tabulation basis may not reflect current results. Rather than trusting the number as is, it is safer to confirm it at the next earnings release.

Shareholder composition

  • Founder Naruatsu Baba (馬場 功淳): 47.4%
  • Baillie Gifford affiliates: & Co. 6.44%, Japanese Fund 3.11%, Japan Trust PLC 2.53%
  • Nomura Asset Management: 1.6%

With the founder holding nearly half of the shares, the free float is small, which explains the thin trading volume. On the other hand, overseas asset managers known for long-term investing are among the top holders. The National Pension Service of Korea does not hold the stock.

Schedule and risks to check

  • The next earnings announcement is scheduled for November 4, 2026. The full-year results for the September fiscal year and the new-title release schedule are the key points.
  • Reliance on non-operating income: you need to see how sustainable core profit is.
  • Reliance on new titles: if releases are delayed or fail to become hits, the sales decline could continue.
  • Low liquidity: because trading value is small, the share price can swing widely.

Summary

The key to COLOPL is checking the quality of the numbers behind "lower sales, higher profit." Whether the profit improvement reflects a real change in the core business or one-off factors, and whether the new titles can reverse the sales decline, will be partly answered at the November earnings release.
