[Today's Stock] Only $125.4M of $126.5M Net Income Came From Insurance? The Real Story Behind Core Natural Resources' EPS Surprise

psythinktank_RA|2026.10.06
조회수 00 views閲覧数 0浏览量 0

EPS of $2.51, But the Market Expected $0.60

U.S. coal company Core Natural Resources (NYSE: CNR) reported diluted earnings per share (EPS) of $2.51 for the second quarter on August 6. That is far above the market consensus of about $0.60. However, when you break down this "surprise," it is a mix of genuine improvement in the core business and one-time factors. This article walks through what is behind it.

The Numbers First

The Key Variable: Leer South Insurance Proceeds

Leer South is the company's key mine for metallurgical coal (coal used in steelmaking), and production there was disrupted by an earlier incident. The company settled its insurance claim for this mine at the full policy limit and received a total of $154.5 million over the whole period. Of that, $125.4 million was reflected in Q2 results, and an additional $37.9 million was received in July, the company said.

Put side by side, net income of about $126.5 million and recognized insurance proceeds of $125.4 million suggest that much of the net income headline appears to come from the insurance payout. We could not confirm the exact after-tax contribution, so this is an estimate. Because this type of income does not recur, the $2.51 EPS should not be treated as a normal run rate going forward. It is reasonable to view the current price-to-earnings ratio (PER) of around 46x as distorted for the same reason.

Still, the Core Business Improved

  • High-CV Thermal coal (high-calorific thermal coal): Sales volume rose 9% from the prior quarter to 8.4 million tons, and cash cost per ton fell 9% to $38.58.
  • Metallurgical coal (steelmaking coal): Sales volume rose 8% to 2.3 million tons, and cash cost per ton fell 7% to $85.65.
  • Powder River Basin (PRB): Sales volume declined to 10.2 million tons, and cash margin per ton was -$0.57, a loss. Lower volumes and higher fuel costs were cited as causes.

In short, the two higher-margin segments improved, while PRB, which produces lower-calorific coal, is dragging results down.

What to Watch Next

  • Forward sales commitments: The company secured new sales contracts for 16 million tons of future deliveries.
  • 2026 guidance: Total sales volume of 87.3 million to 92.4 million tons, and capital expenditures of $325 million to $375 million.
  • Liquidity: About $1 billion as of the end of June.
  • Shareholder returns: About $360 million cumulatively since February 2025. The quarterly dividend is $0.10 per share ($0.40 per year), a dividend yield of only about 0.4%.
  • Management change: Nathan Tucker was appointed as the new CFO (chief financial officer) in August. UBS downgraded its rating from "Buy" to "Neutral" on August 18.
  • Clues on new businesses: The U.S. Department of Energy selected the company's innovation unit for a project to extract critical minerals from coal, and its subsidiary Touchstone took part in developing a Northrop Grumman unmanned aircraft in aerospace composites. Their contribution to earnings is still small, so they are long-term watch items.
  • Next earnings release: Scheduled for November 5. It will be the first time the "pure core business" results, without insurance proceeds, are revealed.

Where the Stock Stands

The October 5 close was $91.01 (+2.3% from the previous day), with a market capitalization of about $4.5 billion and a 52-week range of $74.75 to $114.80. According to the data provided, the one-year price change is -2.6%, little changed, and the current price is about 20% below the 52-week high. The analyst average price target is about $109.5 to $115 depending on the source, above the current price, but a price target is only a forecast, not a guaranteed figure.

The main shareholders are mostly index-tracking funds: BlackRock (14.12%), State Street (7.96%), iShares S&P Small-Cap ETF (6.19%) and Vanguard (6.08%). The hedge fund Greenlight Capital holds 4.6%. No holding by Korea's National Pension Service was confirmed.

Summary

This quarter's EPS was greatly inflated by a one-time factor, the insurance proceeds. That does not mean the core business is weak: lower costs and higher volumes in the High-CV Thermal and metallurgical coal segments are a clear improvement. Going forward, the key issues are eliminating the PRB loss, the steelmaking coal market, and earnings power after the insurance proceeds.
