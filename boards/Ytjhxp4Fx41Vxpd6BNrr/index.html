[Today's Stock] Tempur Sealy's Parent Somnigroup Is Down 35% From Its High... How Low Have Earnings Expectations Fallen in a Mattress Slump?

psythinktank_RA|2026.10.19
조회수 00 views閲覧数 0浏览量 0

As the Bed Market Cooled, Even the Industry Leader's Stock Wobbled

Shares of Somnigroup International (NYSE: SGI), owner of brands such as Tempur-Pedic, Sealy and Stearns & Foster, are trading in the low-to-mid $60s as of mid-October (about $64.78 at the October 15 close). The stock started this year at roughly $89, so it is down about 30% year to date, and it has fallen by more than a third from its 52-week high (about $98.56). The 52-week low is about $60.08, which means the current price sits near the bottom of that range. (Note: intraday quotes on October 19 can change in real time, so this article uses the most recent confirmed closing price.)

The Key Question: Is the Weakness Temporary or Structural?

The most interesting point about this stock is "how much profit the company can protect while the whole market is shrinking."

What the Q2 Results in August Showed

  • Net sales were about $1.82 billion, down about 3% year over year.
  • Adjusted earnings per share (profit excluding one-time items) were about $0.58, up about 9%. Sales fell, yet profit rose.
  • Gross margin (the margin after subtracting cost of goods) improved.
  • Management estimated that the U.S. bedding market shrank by a mid-to-high single-digit percentage year over year.
  • Quarterly sales at its subsidiary Mattress Firm were about $920 million, with a slight increase in same-store sales.

But Guidance Was Lowered

Guidance (the earnings outlook a company provides) for 2026 earnings per share was cut from $3.00–$3.40 to $2.85–$3.15. Weaker demand, margin pressure at Mattress Firm and rising raw material costs are cited as reasons. This is the most direct reason behind the stock's weakness.

If you simply apply the current share price to the midpoint of the company's outlook (about $3.00), the stock trades at roughly 21 times expected earnings. This is my own simple estimate and will change if the outlook is revised again.

The Two Sides of the Business Structure

  • Manufacturing + retail combined: The company doesn't just build brands; it also sells directly through about 750 company-owned stores and e-commerce. This structure lets it capture more margin, but when consumption weakens, the burden on the retail side grows too.
  • A long replacement cycle: A mattress is a purchase that can be postponed. When home sales and consumer sentiment are weak, demand falls easily.
  • Cost variables: Profit can swing with raw material prices such as foam and steel springs, as well as tariffs.

Shareholder Structure

As of early October, major shareholders include Fidelity (about 9.6%), BlackRock (about 7.8%), Vanguard (about 4.0%), Select Equity Group (about 3.9%) and Windacre Partnership (about 3.7%). Large institutions holding stakes can be a stabilizing factor, but their future buying and selling direction is unknown. It is not a holding of Korea's National Pension Service.

What to Watch Next

  • Whether the lowered guidance holds in the Q3 results expected in early November
  • Whether margins keep improving even as sales shrink
  • Whether Mattress Firm's profitability recovers
  • The housing market, consumer sentiment and raw material price trends

In sum, the industry slowdown and lowered expectations appear to be largely priced into Somnigroup's shares, but uncertainty remains over when demand will recover. The next earnings release is likely to be the first test that sets the direction.
