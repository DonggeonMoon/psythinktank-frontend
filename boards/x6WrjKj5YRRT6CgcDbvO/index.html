[Today's Stock] M3 Owns 55% of Hospital Linen Rental Firm Elan (6099) — and Part of Its Profit Jump Came from an 'Accounting Change'

psythinktank_RA|2026.11.07
조회수 00 views閲覧数 0浏览量 0

The company that rents hospital gowns and towels: Elan (6099)

Elan (エラン, 6099) is a Japanese company that rents bundled sets of patient gowns, towels and daily necessities to hospitals, nursing care facilities and group homes, and also handles the laundry. Its core service is the "CS Set," a subscription-style model in which facilities pay monthly and don't need to buy or launder items themselves. As the number of facilities grows, revenue accumulates, so earnings are relatively predictable.

This article's theme: the 'depreciation method change' hidden inside the profit growth

In the first half of this year, Elan's revenue rose about 10% year on year and operating profit rose about 28.3%. The main driver of revenue growth was an increase in the number of facilities under domestic CS Set contracts.

However, accounting also played a part in why operating profit grew much faster than revenue. According to the company, from January this year it changed how it expenses its "lifte" patient gowns, from "expensing everything at purchase" to "depreciating over three years." Depreciation is an accounting treatment that spreads the purchase cost of a long-lived asset over its period of use as expenses. This change doesn't mean more cash is coming in; it means the timing of expense recognition is spread out, which boosts short-term profit. So when looking at profit growth, it is safer to separate real operating growth from the effect of the accounting change.

  • At the end-of-July first-half results, the full-year forecast was left unchanged.
  • Some assessments, however, said first-half profit fell short of consensus (the average analyst forecast).
  • Third-quarter results are confirmed to have been released on October 30, but the detailed figures could not be verified in this research. Please check them directly in timely disclosures (TDnet) before investing.

The presence of top shareholder M3

M3, a medical information platform company, acquired about 55% of Elan through a 2024 tender offer (TOB, a method of publicly buying shares outside the market) and made it a subsidiary. The offer price was 1,040 yen per share, and the listing was maintained. The current share price is far below that level. The one-year price change is also about -5.5%.

Other major shareholders include Hideharu Sakurai at 4.47%, Nobuhiro Nakajima at 4%, Sakura Co., Ltd. at 3.04%, and Polar Capital Japan Value Fund at 1.57%. Because large shareholders hold a big share, the number of shares available for trading is limited, which is worth keeping in mind from a liquidity standpoint. The median annual trading value is about 70.5 million yen. This figure comes from the provided data, and since nothing has been confirmed about future cooperation with the parent company or its shareholding policy, I will refrain from speculation.
