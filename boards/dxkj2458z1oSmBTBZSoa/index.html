[Today's Stock] A $400M Company With Revenue Doubled? Columbus McKinnon, and the Price of Bulk Bought With Debt

Revenue Doubled, Share Price Stuck: The Mixed Numbers of Columbus McKinnon (CMCO)

Columbus McKinnon, which makes hoists (equipment for lifting heavy loads), chains, and lifting components, acquired Kito Crosby, a large competitor and complementary player, in February 2026. As a result, quarterly revenue more than doubled, but a book net loss and high debt came along with it. Today's post focuses on one question: "Why is the share price of a company that has grown so much still so quiet?"

Current Share Price Situation

  • Share price: about $14.6 (intraday on 2026-10-21; the close on the 22nd is not yet confirmed, so the most recent trading value was used)
  • Market cap: around $400 million (sources differ, ranging from $420 million to $470 million)
  • 52-week range: about $11.99 to $24.40
  • Dividend: $0.28 per quarter (this is $0.28 per share, not an annualized figure of about $1.10; some sources list a dividend yield of about 1.9%, so this needs to be verified)
  • Next earnings release: expected in early November (some sites list November 3, but it may not yet be officially confirmed by the company)

Latest Quarter (Fiscal 2027 Q1, April–June) Report Card

ItemFigure
Revenue$531.5 million (+125% year over year)
Orders$568.1 million (+120%), book-to-bill ratio of 1.1x
BacklogAbout $541 million (+50%)
Adjusted EBITDA$111.5 million, 21% margin
Adjusted earnings per share (EPS)$0.61
GAAP net lossAbout $88.7 million (-$2.05 per share)

EBITDA is earnings before interest, taxes, and depreciation and amortization, a measure of how much cash the core business generates. Adjusted figures exclude one-time items such as acquisition and integration costs, while the GAAP net loss includes those costs (about $70.3 million) in full. In other words, a large part of the "loss" is acquisition cost, but that cost is still real money that left the company.

Why the Market Hesitates, Reason 1: Debt

The acquisition was mostly financed with borrowing. The company said its net debt/EBITDA ratio (a multiple showing how many years of operating earnings it would take to repay the debt) fell to about 4.9x at the end of Q1, and it aims to bring it below 4x by fiscal 2028. A third-party source reported total debt in the mid-$2 billion range. With a market cap of around $400 million, the shareholders' slice of the company's total value is small and the creditors' slice is much larger. In this kind of structure, even a modest improvement in the business can move the share price sharply, but a weak performance can also cause a bigger drop (this is a general interpretation of the structure).

Why the Market Hesitates, Reason 2: The Warning That "Q2 Is the Low Point"

  • Management said Q2 (July–September) would be the low point of the year for revenue and adjusted EBITDA.
  • It explained that of the roughly 300bp (3 percentage points) of margin improvement in Q1, about 200bp came from one-off factors such as raw material cost benefits and a temporary increase in shipments to the Middle East.
  • Demand in Europe, the Middle East, and Africa (EMEA) was weak, while the Americas and Asia-Pacific were strong.

That is why "where the low point turned out to be" is the key thing to watch in the upcoming early-November results.

Positives

  • Raised full-year guidance (company outlook): revenue of $2.09 billion to $2.15 billion, adjusted EBITDA of $405 million to $420 million, adjusted EPS of $1.90 to $2.10
  • Management expressed confidence it could exceed its annual cost-savings (synergy) target of $70 million
  • Growing backlog and a 1.1x book-to-bill ratio are favorable for future revenue visibility
  • Potential beneficiary of spending on infrastructure, power, and manufacturing equipment (the Global X US Infrastructure Development ETF is among the major shareholders at about 9.5%)

Gauging It With Numbers (My Own Calculation, for Reference)

Dividing the current share price of $14.6 by the midpoint of the company's adjusted EPS guidance, about $2.0, gives an adjusted P/E of about 7x. However, this is based on adjusted earnings presented by the company, which exclude acquisition costs and amortization of intangible assets, so it differs significantly from GAAP earnings. In addition, last fiscal year included a roughly $200 million non-cash goodwill impairment charge tied to the share price decline, which produced a large net loss. The low multiple can be read as a sign that the market is discounting "the likelihood of meeting guidance and the debt burden."

Shareholder Composition

Based on recent filings, institutional holdings are large, including BlackRock-affiliated entities at about 7.1%, Mirae Asset Global Investments (USA) at about 6.7%, Dimensional at about 4.4%, and Vanguard Capital Management at about 4.2%. There has been no change in these stakes over the past few weeks. Holdings by the National Pension Service of Korea have not been confirmed.

Checkpoints

  • Q2 results in early November: are the revenue and EBITDA lows within the guidance range?
  • Does the net debt ratio fall further from 4.9x?
  • Does the EMEA weakness drag on, and do acquisition synergies come through as expected?
  • Does the second-half margin recovery scenario (management's outlook) materialize?
