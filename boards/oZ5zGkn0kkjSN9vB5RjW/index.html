Record Earnings, but the Stock Is Stalling: What Is Going On at Sanrio?

Where Is Sanrio's Stock Price Right Now?

Sanrio (8136) closed at 1,212 yen on September 24. Its 52-week range runs from 818.8 yen to 1,594 yen. The current price sits about 24% below the 52-week high and about 48% above the 52-week low.

The September 3 close was 1,288.5 yen, so the stock has slipped roughly another 6% over the past three weeks. The shares that plunged after the August earnings release have failed to rebound and have lost momentum again.

What Kind of Company Is Sanrio?

Sanrio is a Japanese company that owns character intellectual property (IP) such as Hello Kitty, My Melody, Kuromi, and Cinnamoroll. It was founded in 1960 and is listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange.

It used to be seen mainly as a company that made and sold merchandise itself. Today, the core of its profit is licensing, where other companies pay royalties to use its characters. Inventory risk is small and margins are high, so profit tends to grow faster than sales when sales rise.

Earnings Are Still at Record Levels

In the interim results for the fiscal year ended March 2026, sales rose 39.6% year on year to 87.6 billion yen, and operating profit rose 66.1% to 39.1 billion yen. The operating margin was 44.7%. The key point is that profit grew much faster than sales.

Full-year operating profit for the fiscal year ended March 2026 reached 77.9 billion yen, up 50% from the previous year. The company forecasts operating profit of 89.5 billion yen for the fiscal year ending March 2027, which is a 15% increase.

By region, sales grew 128% in Europe, 75% in South America, and 63.2% in Asia. In North America, sales rose 16.5% but profit fell 10.5%. Growth overseas is clearly uneven.

Why Did the Stock Fall?

The first trigger was a broad correction in character-related stocks. As the craze for Pop Mart's Labubu in China cooled, selling hit related names, and analysts said Sanrio was caught in the spillover. At one point the stock fell to about half of its peak.

The second trigger was the first-quarter report in August. After Sanrio released results for the first quarter of the fiscal year ending March 2027 on August 12, 2026, the stock dropped about 18% in a single day. Net profit rose 9% year on year to 15.5 billion yen, a record for the fourth year in a row, but operating profit fell short of market expectations. Investors read it as a sign that growth was slowing, even though it was still continuing.

When expectations are high, even good results cannot protect a stock price. Sanrio's recent trading is a textbook example.

The Company's Response: Shareholder Returns and M&A

In its medium-term plan, Sanrio set aside a 50 billion yen investment budget for M&A and capital alliances. When no large deal met its criteria, the company said at its June earnings briefing that it would consider up to 30 billion yen in additional shareholder returns if it could not find a suitable acquisition.

In February 2026 it announced a 5-for-1 stock split, signaling a wish to broaden its investor base. In August it also disclosed a plan to grant restricted treasury shares to more than 1,100 employees. An interim dividend of 8 yen has been set, with an ex-dividend date of September 29.

Analysts Are Still Constructive

The average 12-month price target is about 1,554 yen, roughly 28% above the current price. In late August J.P. Morgan maintained a buy rating, and Iwai Cosmo Securities also rated the stock a buy. Okasan Securities kept a hold rating, so views are not fully aligned.

However, price targets rest on the assumption that earnings come in as expected. If the next results again fall short of expectations, targets could be lowered as well.

Points to Watch From an Investment Perspective

  • Thanks to its licensing-centered structure, margins are high and profit grows faster than sales when sales increase.

  • The most important factor is whether growth in Europe, Asia, and South America holds up.

  • We need to check whether the decline in North American profit is temporary or structural.

  • Character-related stocks are sensitive to trends, so when the craze for a particular IP fades, the whole sector can wobble.

  • Whether the M&A deal happens or the company shifts to shareholder returns will be the next stock catalyst.

  • A P/E of 27x is not low even allowing for high growth, so the stock may be sensitive to any slowdown.

Upcoming Schedule to Check

The next earnings release is scheduled for late October to early November. Sources list different dates, October 30 and November 6, so it is safer to confirm the official investor relations schedule. The key question is whether this release eases the growth-slowdown worries from August.

Summary

Sanrio is a representative case of earnings and share price moving in opposite directions. Profit keeps setting records, but as high expectations were dented, the stock now sits 24% below its high. From here, what matters is whether the growth rate holds and how shareholder returns and M&A play out.
