[Today's Stock] 70% of the Year's Profit Packed Into the Second Half? Japan IT Mid-Cap ITFOR's Back-Loaded Earnings Plan

조회수 00 views閲覧数 0浏览量 0

Earnings Pushed Into the Second Half: Why This Interim Report Matters

ITFOR (アイティフォー, 4743), listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange, is a Japanese IT services company that handles debt-collection management systems for financial institutions, network and security solutions, and POS systems for retailers. This article looks at how heavily this year's earnings plan leans on the second half.

1. Current Share Price and Valuation

  • The latest confirmed closing prices are in the mid-1,500 yen range: about 1,571 yen on November 4 and 1,535 yen as of November 6. In mid-September it was in the mid-1,600 yen range. November 15 is a Sunday, so there is no new closing price, and I could not confirm intraday quotes.
  • The one-year share price change is about -7.5%.
  • The company's forecast earnings per share for this fiscal year (ending March 2027) is 128 yen. At 1,535 yen, the price-to-earnings ratio (PER, an indicator showing how many times earnings the share price represents) is about 12x. This figure assumes the company's forecast is achieved.
  • The forecast annual dividend is 80 yen, giving a dividend yield of about 5.2% at 1,535 yen. The dividend is a company plan, not a confirmed amount.

2. This Year's Plan: Sales of 28.0 Billion Yen, Ordinary Profit of 4.9 Billion Yen

Results for the previous fiscal year (ended March 2026) were sales of about 23.1 billion yen and ordinary profit (profit that also reflects non-operating income and expenses) of about 4.05 billion yen. The company's plan for this year is as follows.

ItemCompany forecast for this year
Sales28.0 billion yen (approx. +21%)
Operating profit4.8 billion yen (approx. +24%)
Ordinary profit4.9 billion yen (approx. +21%)
Net profit3.4 billion yen (approx. +23%)

3. Where the Skew Shows

The first quarter (April–June), announced on August 12, showed sales of 4.62 billion yen (+9.2%), operating profit of 555 million yen (+28.5%), and ordinary profit of 509 million yen (+3.5%). Progress against the full-year ordinary profit plan is only about 10%. Some assessments also said it fell short of market consensus (the average forecast of professional analysts).

The company's first-half ordinary profit forecast is 1.45 billion yen. Subtracting that from the annual 4.9 billion yen leaves about 3.45 billion yen for the second half, meaning roughly 70% of annual profit is concentrated there. First-half sales are forecast at 10.8 billion yen, so about 17.2 billion yen is needed in the second half. Many Japanese companies have March fiscal year-ends, and deliveries and acceptance inspections tend to cluster toward year-end, so this kind of seasonality is not unusual in this industry. However, the risk of missing the plan grows accordingly.

4. Checkpoints

  • Interim results: The interim results announcement was scheduled for November 12. Within the scope of my search, I could not confirm the actual figures. It is important to check whether first-half ordinary profit came in around the planned 1.45 billion yen.
  • Second-half order backlog and delivery schedule: Whether the back-loaded plan is realistic depends on this.
  • Gap with consensus: The analyst average for ordinary profit is about 4.65 billion yen, lower than the company plan (4.9 billion yen). This suggests the market views the plan as somewhat optimistic.
  • Dividend policy: An annual 80 yen is expected, including a 40 yen interim dividend. It is worth watching whether this level is maintained.

5. Shareholder Composition

As of October 2026, major shareholders include Hikari Tsushin (6.23%), Sumitomo Mitsui DS Asset Management (6%), the employee shareholding association (3.87%), Mitsubishi UFJ Trust and Banking (2.48%), and Nomura Asset Management (2.24%). The National Pension Service of Korea appears not to hold shares. This is a small-cap stock with a median annual trading value of around 110 million yen, so liquidity may be low.

Summary

A PER of around 12x and a dividend yield in the 5% range look attractive on the numbers alone. But these figures hold only if the plan for a sharp profit increase in the second half is realized. The share price will likely depend on second-half progress after the interim results.
