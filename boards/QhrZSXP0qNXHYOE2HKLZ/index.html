[Today's Stock] Is the -67% Drop an Illusion? The Real Numbers Behind NICE Information & Telecommunication's Stock Split

psythinktank_RA|2026.10.02
조회수 00 views閲覧数 0浏览量 0

Let's Doubt the "-67% in One Year" Figure First

According to the data, the one-year price change of NICE Information & Telecommunication (036800, KOSDAQ) is -67.52%. The 52-week high is 42,300 won, and the current price as of October 2 is 7,910 won (+2.59% from the previous day), about 81% below the peak. But taking this figure at face value would be a mistake, because a stock split took place earlier this year.

A stock split divides one share into several shares to lower the per-share price, and it does not change the value of the company itself. NICE Information & Telecommunication changed its par value from 500 won to 100 won, increasing the number of shares outstanding fivefold, from about 9.1 million to 45.5 million. If you compare the pre-split price directly with the post-split price, the decline looks larger than it really is. I could not confirm the exact adjusted price, but the current market capitalization is about 359.9 billion won, versus about 178.4 billion won in March this year, when an activist fund was raising objections. On that basis, the company's value appears to have actually risen (estimate).

The stock trades at a low level relative to both earnings and net assets. However, low multiples do not automatically mean undervaluation. The market may be applying a discount for good reason.

June 2: Two Disclosures on the Same Day

The most interesting part of this stock is the two decisions announced on June 2 alone.

1) Shareholder return package

  • Decision to cancel (retire) about 900,000 treasury shares worth 14.7 billion won. Cancellation means the company destroys shares it has bought back, raising the value of the remaining shares.
  • A 3 billion won treasury share purchase trust contract
  • Stock split (500 won → 100 won)

2) Acquisition of affiliate KIS Information & Communications

  • Acquisition of a 96.1% stake in KIS Information & Communications held by the largest shareholder, NICE Holdings, for about 112.3 billion won (scheduled acquisition date: July 2)
  • Media outlets pointed out that the cash flowing to the holding company is far larger than the shareholder return amount (about 17.7 billion won), and that the price is about 26.8 billion won higher than NICE Holdings' book value (about 85.5 billion won), so the grounds for it need to be verified.

In other words, a signal of "giving back to shareholders" and a signal of "cash leaving through an intra-group transaction" came out at the same time. That is why market opinion is divided.

Why Is So Much Being Said About Cash?

Ahead of this year's March shareholders' meeting, the activist fund Value Partners criticized the company for holding more cash than its market capitalization (about 332.5 billion won in cash and cash equivalents on a consolidated basis, and about 352.8 billion won in net assets as of the end of 2024) while its PBR stayed around 0.5x. It also pointed out that share buybacks over the past three years came to only about 15 billion won, and that the treasury shares it holds (about 12.6%) had not been cancelled. The latest cancellation decision can be seen as a partial response to those demands.

Business Structure: Payments Under One Roof

Founded in 1988, it is a payments specialist within the NICE Group and was listed on KOSDAQ in 2000. It spun off its online payment (PG) business in 2016 to create NICE Payments, then merged it back in 2025 to consolidate the business.

  • PG (online payment gateway): about 60.4% of revenue
  • VAN (relaying approvals between card terminals and card companies): about 34.8%
  • POS and others: about 4.8%

VAN growth is limited by government fee regulation, while PG benefits from the expansion of online payments. Last year, expectations of an Apple Pay expansion were also reflected in the share price.

Notable Changes in Shareholder Composition

  • NICE Holdings and one other party: 46.93% (as of September 21, 2026). Up from 42.7% at the end of 2025, which may partly reflect the effect of the total share count shrinking after the treasury share cancellation (estimate).
  • Fidelity Management and 6 others: 6.22%
  • South China Securities: 5.01%

The National Pension Service is confirmed not to hold a stake. As the largest shareholder's stake rises, the free float shrinks. Note also that the annual median trading value is only about 360 million won, making this a thinly traded stock.

How to Read the Dividend and Growth Figures

The data shows a dividend of 1,100 won, but this is likely based on a record date before the stock split. Converted to post-split terms, it could be around 220 won per share (estimate; the exact value needs to be confirmed through disclosures). The 87.83% growth rate can also be interpreted differently depending on which metric it is based on, so it is hard to judge on its own.

Points to Watch Going Forward

  • Whether PG segment growth and margins hold up in second-quarter and later results
  • The earnings contribution on a consolidated basis after the KIS Information & Communications acquisition, and whether the controversy over the appropriateness of the price is resolved
  • Where the cash goes next: additional buybacks and cancellations, higher dividends, and so on
  • Reduced floating shares and sluggish trading as the largest shareholder's stake rises

To sum up, the -67% figure is very likely an illusion created by the stock split, and the real issue is "for whom the abundant cash will be used." Because willingness to return value to shareholders and an intra-group transaction coexist in the same disclosure, the company's next moves are likely to shape how it is judged.
