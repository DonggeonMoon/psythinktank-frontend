[Today's Stock] More Engineers Signed Up, but Too Few Were Put to Work: Two Stone & Sons Down 58% in a Year

psythinktank_RA|2026.11.16
조회수 00 views閲覧数 0浏览量 0

Why Sign-ups Grew but Sales Fell Short of the Plan

Two Stone & Sons (TSE Growth 7352), which runs the IT freelance engineer matching platform "Midworks," lowered its earnings forecast on October 6, 2026. The share price is about 58% lower than a year ago (data as of 2026-11-06). Around the 2026-11-13 close, the stock was about 303 yen, with a market capitalization of about 13.5 billion yen. This article looks at why earnings fell short even though the number of people grew.

What Kind of Company Is It?

  • Engineer platform service: The Midworks business connects IT engineers with companies that need development talent.
  • Marketing platform service: It operates information media aimed at IT engineers and practitioners.
  • Recently, it has also been investing in consulting/advisory and AI consulting businesses.

Details of the Downward Revision for the Fiscal Year Ending August 2026

Sales rose about 20% from the prior year, so the business grew in size, but operating profit was essentially flat (810 vs. 819) and net income declined. Compared with the previous forecast, operating profit is about 39% lower.

The Key Is the Number of Active Engineers

The company said the cause was that splitting its sales function by specialty and refining project management took longer than planned. Its ability to make proposals to companies and the accuracy of matching projects with engineers also remain challenges.

  • Active engineers: 22,684 vs. the 25,134 expected at the start of the year
  • Registered engineers: 71,258 vs. the 68,000 expected at the start of the year

In short, the company gathered enough people to work, but it could not connect them to actual projects fast enough. In this business, engineers placed on projects are what generate sales, so the number of active engineers, not sign-ups, drives results. A decline in sales of high-margin marketing products and upfront costs for consulting staff and the AI business also weighed on profit.

The Company's Plan for Next Year Is Aggressive

In its full-year results announcement on October 15, the company presented targets for the fiscal year ending August 2027.

  • Sales of 25,198 million yen (up about 16% from this year's result)
  • Operating profit of 1,531 million yen (up about 89%)
  • Ordinary profit of 1,482 million yen (up about 75%)
  • Net income of 774 million yen

The market consensus (average of brokerage forecasts) for ordinary profit is 1,350 million yen, below the company's target. Having just missed its target by a wide margin, the company appears not to be fully trusted by the market on its plan.

Valuation at a Glance

  • Forecast PER (price-to-earnings ratio, the share price relative to profit): about 17.5x (based on company forecast)
  • Actual PBR (price-to-book ratio): about 3.5x
  • Forecast dividend yield: about 0.33%

If profit reaches the company's target, the burden is not heavy, but if it misses, the same share price can look expensive. In other words, the stock's valuation could vary widely depending on whether the plan is achieved.

Shareholder Structure

As of October 1, 2026, the major shareholders are Yasuyuki Kawabata at 31.26% and Katsuya Takahara at 31.19%, so the two founding executives together hold over 60%. Others include Sumitomo Mitsui Trust Bank at 9.85%, Masashi Kurata at 6.51%, and Mynavi at 3.49%. Compared with the September 20 data, most holdings edged down slightly, such as Kawabata (31.40%→31.26%) and Takahara (31.33%→31.19%), but the changes are small and it is hard to draw conclusions. No holding by the National Pension Service of Korea was confirmed. Because major shareholders hold a large share, the free float is small, and the median trading value is small at about 74.81 million yen per year, so the share price may be volatile.

What to Watch Next

  • How quickly the quarterly number of active engineers rises from 22,684
  • Whether the sales reorganization leads to better matching accuracy
  • How much new investment costs such as AI consulting weigh on profit
  • Whether the gap between the company target (ordinary profit of 1,482 million yen) and consensus narrows
