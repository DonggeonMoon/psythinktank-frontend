[Today's Stock] Market Cap 51.7B Won but Equity 92.9B? GI Tech at a 52-Week Low: What the Q3 Loss Really Means

psythinktank_RA|2026.11.21
조회수 00 views閲覧数 0浏览量 0

A Q3 "Earnings Shock" at the 52-Week Low

GI Tech (382480), a KOSDAQ-listed maker of electrode-coating components for secondary batteries, has posted a Q3 loss. Its share price has fallen close to its 52-week low. The writing date (November 21) is a Saturday, so the prices below are based on the regular session of the previous trading day, November 20.

ItemDetails
Current price (11/20)1,288 won (-2.05% from previous day)
Market capApprox. 51.7 billion won
52-week range1,245 won ~ 3,265 won
Largest shareholder and related parties47.76% (as of 2026-10-31)

The price is about 60% below its 52-week high. The share price change over the past year is around -33%.

Q3 Numbers: Revenue Down 47%, Operating Loss Widens

  • Revenue: approx. 3.4 billion won (-47% year over year)
  • Operating loss: approx. 2.7 billion won (the loss widened from about 1.4 billion won a year earlier)
  • Net loss: approx. 6.0 billion won (a small profit a year earlier)
  • January–September cumulative: revenue of approx. 23.1 billion won, similar to the previous year; operating loss of approx. 2.7 billion won

The company explains that delivery and inspection for some projects scheduled for September slipped into Q4. In the equipment business, revenue is recognized only after delivery and inspection are complete, so a one-quarter delay can swing that quarter's results sharply. This is the company's explanation, and whether results actually recover in Q4 remains to be seen.

Much of the 6.0 Billion Won Net Loss Is an "FX Valuation Loss"

The net loss is much larger than the operating loss mainly because of an FX valuation loss of approx. 4.1 billion won on its US dollar assets. When the dollar assets it holds were converted into won, their value fell and a loss was booked. According to the company, it is not a cash outflow unless the dollars are actually sold at a lower exchange rate. However, the operating loss itself (approx. 2.7 billion won) is a separate issue. Gross profit shrank to approx. 0.9 billion won while selling and administrative expenses rose 29% to approx. 3.6 billion won. This shows a structure in which fixed costs keep going out even when revenue falls.

An Interesting Point: Equity Larger Than Market Cap

Total equity at the end of September was reported at approx. 92.9 billion won. Compared simply with the market cap of approx. 51.7 billion won, the PBR (price-to-book ratio, market cap divided by net assets) is roughly 0.56x. That means the market is valuing the company at only a bit over half of its book assets. The two figures have different reference dates, and equity may include intangible assets such as goodwill recognized from the merger, so these numbers alone cannot justify calling the stock "undervalued." This part is an estimate.

Financial stability is not bad. On a standalone basis, the debt ratio is 35% and the current ratio (liquid assets relative to debts due within a year) is 198%.

Business Structure: From Component Maker to "Total Solution"

GI Tech was founded in 2012 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2021. It makes slot dies and slit nozzles (ultra-precision parts that apply liquid in a thin, uniform layer) used to coat battery electrodes, and also offers repair services for worn parts. It acquired MVTech, an inspection and vision equipment company, in 2024 and completed the merger in 2025, adding machine vision (technology that finds defects using cameras and AI) inspection solutions to its components.

Annual revenue in 2025 was approx. 36.2 billion won, down 4% from the prior year, and operating profit fell sharply to approx. 1.1 billion won. This means the merger's benefits have not yet shown up in profit.

Ongoing Momentum: Auto Glass Inspection Order

In October, the company announced a 3.15 billion won contract for automotive glass defect inspection equipment. The amount is similar to a full quarter's Q3 revenue, so it is meaningful. Still, how much slot die demand will recover during the slowdown in battery investment, and whether vision inspection will become a new revenue source, are separate challenges.

Checkpoints

  • Q4 results: whether the deferred deliveries are actually reflected in revenue
  • SG&A control: a structure in which losses can continue if revenue does not recover
  • Exchange rate: valuation gains and losses from holding dollar assets
  • Order growth: additional contracts outside batteries, such as auto glass inspection
  • Ownership structure: the largest shareholder side holds 47.76% with no recent change, and treasury shares were 2.05% as of April 2025.
  • Dividend: 7 won per share, a negligible amount.

Summary

On the numbers alone, this Q3 is weak. However, part of the revenue decline is a delivery timing delay, and a large share of the net loss is an FX valuation loss with no cash outflow. On the other hand, the SG&A burden, dependence on the battery industry, and the absence of profit improvement after the merger remain unresolved. Whether the deferred revenue shows up in Q4 is the nearest checkpoint.
