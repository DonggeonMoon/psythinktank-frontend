[Today's Stock] Sales Up, Profit Down: The 'Second-Half Homework' Facing Japan's Baby Goods Leader Nishimatsuya

psythinktank_RA|2026.11.06
조회수 00 views閲覧数 0浏览量 0

First-Half Report Card: Selling More, Keeping Less

Nishimatsuya Chain (西松屋チェーン, 7545), Japan's specialty chain for children's clothing and baby products, announced its interim results (March–August) for the fiscal year ending February 2027 on October 1. The key point is "higher sales, lower profit." These numbers are the best starting point for reading this stock right now.

ItemFirst-half resultYear-on-year
Net sales¥102.636 billion (1,026億3,600万 yen)+5.8%
Operating profit¥7.032 billion (70億3,200万 yen)-3.7%
Ordinary profit¥7.378 billion (73億7,800万 yen)-1.7%
Net profit¥4.992 billion (49億9,200万 yen)+0.5%

Ordinary profit is operating profit plus non-operating items such as interest and dividend income. It is the metric most often cited in Japanese earnings announcements.

Why Did Profit Fall?

The company said sales rose thanks to growth in clothing and miscellaneous goods. On the other hand, it opened 34 new stores in the first half, which raised selling, general and administrative expenses (rent, labor, advertising and so on) and pushed down operating and ordinary profit. A new store incurs costs right away, while its sales take time to stabilize. That is why profit is often temporarily squeezed during periods of aggressive store openings. How quickly the new stores actually settle in remains to be seen.

The Real Focus: How Much Must Profit Jump in the Second Half?

The company did not change its full-year plan.

  • Full-year sales: ¥205 billion
  • Full-year operating profit: ¥12.54 billion
  • Full-year ordinary profit: ¥13 billion (about 23% higher than the previous year)

The first-half ordinary profit of ¥7.378 billion is about 56.8% of the full-year plan. The following are my own calculations based on published figures.

  • Ordinary profit for the previous fiscal year (ended February 2026) was about ¥10.57 billion. Since this year's first half fell 1.7%, last year's first half works out to roughly ¥7.5 billion. The previous second half is therefore estimated to have been in the ¥3 billion range.
  • To reach ¥13 billion for the year, the company needs to earn about ¥5.6 billion in the second half. That is well above last year's second half, roughly an 80% increase.

The analyst consensus (the average of expert forecasts) for ordinary profit is around ¥12.5 billion, slightly below the company plan. In other words, the market also seems to think that "hitting the plan won't be easy." The first-half progress rate also fell short of the five-year average (about 60%).

A Positive Sign: September Existing-Store Sales

According to reports, September sales at existing stores (stores open for more than a year) rose in the 12% range year on year. This means same-store sales, excluding the effect of new stores, were strong. It could be a basis for a second-half profit recovery. However, it is hard to call a trend from a single month, so we need to see whether the monthly figures continue from October onward.

Shareholder Returns: Higher Dividend and a Buyback

  • The interim dividend was raised to ¥17, and the full-year dividend was increased to ¥33.
  • The company also announced a share buyback of up to ¥300 million. The size is small, so it is closer to a signal of commitment to shareholder returns than something that will move the share price much.

Against the latest confirmed closing price of ¥1,870 (as of October 30), the full-year dividend of ¥33 gives a dividend yield of about 1.8%.

Where Is the Share Price Now?

  • Latest confirmed price: ¥1,870 (October 30 close)
  • One-year change: about -10%
  • In mid-April it was in the ¥2,100 range (¥2,135 on April 10), so it is currently lower than then.
  • A theoretical price based on PBR (price-to-book ratio, the share price relative to net assets), calculated by some investment information sites, was around ¥1,988. Calculation methods differ by site, so treat it as a reference only.

Quotes for November 6 were not reflected in the sources I checked, so the price above is the most recent confirmed value.

What Kind of Company Is It?

Nishimatsuya is a specialty store for baby and children's goods, selling kids' clothing, underwear, pajamas, formula and feeding supplies, bedding, baby gear such as car seats and strollers, toys, and gift sets. Its major shareholders are mainly stakes tied to the founding family. The asset management company Yuko Estate holds about 13.8%, and Omura family members individually hold about 7.4%, 5.5% and 2.1%. Goldman Sachs International is tallied as holding about 6.3%. The National Pension Service is confirmed not to hold shares.

Summary: What to Watch Next

  • Whether monthly existing-store sales in the second half keep up the strength seen in September
  • How quickly the 34 new stores contribute to profit
  • Whether a sales mix centered on clothing and miscellaneous goods protects margins
  • Whether the ¥13 billion full-year ordinary profit plan is kept or revised

Even against the structural headwind of a falling birthrate, the key question this second half is whether the strategy of expanding stores and sales turns into enough profit to cover the costs.
