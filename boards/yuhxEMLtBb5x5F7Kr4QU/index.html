[Today's Stock] Revenue Up Just 1%, Yet FFO Jumps 17%: The Numbers Gap at Self-Storage REIT SmartStop

psythinktank_RA|2026.10.08
조회수 00 views閲覧数 0浏览量 0

Revenue Barely Moved, but Per-Share Earnings Grew

What stands out in the second-quarter results of SmartStop Self Storage REIT (NYSE: SMA), a U.S. REIT specializing in self storage (storage units that individuals and small businesses rent by the month), is the gap in growth rates. Same-store revenue rose only about 1%, yet FFO per share grew by double digits. This article looks at where that gap came from and what investors should watch.

A REIT is a company that owns and operates real estate and pays out most of its rental income to shareholders. FFO (funds from operations, a REIT-specific earnings measure similar to operating cash flow) adds depreciation and similar items back to net income to better reflect actual cash-generating power.

Key Q2 2026 Results

ItemDetails
Adjusted FFOAbout $29.3 million, up about $4.9 million year over year
Adjusted FFO per shareAbout $0.49, up about 17.6% year over year

Same-store net operating income (NOI)Up 3.7% year over year
Same-store operating margin67.3%, up about 1.5 percentage points
Average occupancy92.5%, about 0.6% lower than a year earlier
Net income attributable to common stockholdersAbout $11.2 million ($0.20 per share)

Same-store figures compare only properties that were also operated a year earlier, stripping out the effect of acquisitions. Net operating income (NOI) is rental income minus property operating costs.

Sources of Growth: Pricing, Costs, and Expansion

  • Occupancy dipped slightly: Units did not fill up more; profit came from rental rates and cost control.
  • Cost savings: Operating expenses fell 3.4%, which turned 1.3% revenue growth into 3.7% NOI growth.
  • Acquisitions and bridge loans: During the quarter, about $46 million went to acquisitions and bridge financing (short-term loans to other operators).
  • Canadian joint venture: NOI at the Canadian joint venture properties rose 9.4%.

Same-store figures do not fully explain the 17.6% rise in FFO. The remainder likely reflects other factors such as acquisitions and changes in capital structure, but I could not confirm the breakdown.

Company Size

SmartStop owns and manages about 218 properties across 23 U.S. states, Washington, D.C., and Canada. It has roughly 17.6 million rentable square feet and about 156,000 units. Of these, 39 properties are in Canada. It also runs a managed REIT platform business that operates other self-storage programs through subsidiaries.

Share Price and Dividend

  • Share price: Around the low $32 range as of early October. The quote on the 1st was about $32.25; I could not verify today's intraday price.
  • 52-week range: A low of about $29.41 (May this year) and a high of about $39.77 (September last year), as reported. The one-year price change is about -9.8%.
  • Dividend: $1.60 per year, paid monthly. At the price above, the dividend yield is roughly 5%.
  • Guidance: The company is reported to have raised its full-year adjusted FFO per share outlook to $1.98–$2.04. At the $2.01 midpoint, the stock trades at about 16 times FFO, a rough figure I calculated myself.
  • Analysts: Most rate it a buy, with an average price target of about $37. However, some brokerages have lowered their targets.

Major Shareholders

Institutional ownership is large. T. Rowe Price holds about 9.33%, BlackRock about 8.67%, Principal Global Investors about 5.79%, and Vanguard-affiliated entities (Portfolio Management and Capital Management) about 5.24% and 4.28%, respectively. Korea's National Pension Service does not appear to hold shares.

What to Watch

  • Pace of demand recovery: This improvement came from pricing and costs, not occupancy. Check whether occupancy keeps falling.
  • Leverage: Some analyses point to a heavy debt load and weak accounting profitability. Interest costs will vary with the direction of interest rates.
  • Hitting guidance: The same-store revenue growth outlook of 0.5–1.5% is modest. Whether the company reaches its full-year FFO target in the second half is the key.
  • Quality of expansion: Whether acquisitions and bridge loans are profitable investments will show up in future results.

Summary

SmartStop is a case of lifting FFO per share through cost control and expansion in an environment of sluggish revenue growth. Checking the occupancy trend and same-store revenue growth in next quarter's results will help judge whether this improvement continues.
