HNI Acquired Steelcase: The Numbers Got Bigger, but the Stock Has Been Flat for a Year

HNI (NYSE: HNI), a US office furniture company, owns brands such as HON, Allsteel, Kimball, and HBF. In December 2025, it acquired its longtime rival Steelcase. The company's size has changed dramatically, yet the share price is around where it was a year ago. This article looks at how the acquisition is showing up in earnings and how much of it the stock has already priced in.

The stock is well above its 52-week low and a little below its high. Compared with the start of this year (about $42), it is up roughly 10%. Versus one year ago, however, the change is about -1%, which is effectively flat.

Q2 Results: Revenue More Than Doubled, Earnings Beat Expectations

  • Revenue: About $1.5 billion. Revenue added by the Steelcase acquisition alone was about $807 million.
  • Office furniture segment revenue: Up 157% year over year to about $1.3 billion. Organic growth, which excludes the effect of the acquisition, was a modest 0.6%.
  • Adjusted earnings per share (EPS): $1.27, above the market consensus of about $1.04. Adjusted EPS excludes one-time items such as acquisition-related costs.
  • GAAP net income: $51.1 million (diluted EPS of $0.70). Adjusted net income was $92.6 million.

The gap between GAAP and adjusted earnings is large, because acquisition-related costs and amortization of intangible assets weighed on GAAP profit. As a result, the GAAP-based P/E ratio (price-to-earnings, the share price divided by per-share earnings) appears to be in the 800s, but that figure is not meaningful. On adjusted earnings, the forward P/E comes out at around 10x.

Key Issue 1: How Much in Synergies Will Materialize?

The company expects operating income to rise by at least $120 million once the Steelcase integration is fully in place. Synergies are the extra profit gained by combining two companies, cutting duplicate costs, and improving purchasing and production efficiency. The company says the integration is proceeding as planned. However, this is the company's own target, and how much is actually achieved will need to be checked quarter by quarter.

Key Issue 2: Will Demand Turn Around in the Second Half?

The company expects volume growth in the office furniture segment to turn positive starting in the third quarter, and projects that, with volume gains and pricing effects, organic growth in the second half will reach the high single digits. Given that organic growth has so far been near 0%, the second-half figures are the test of this investment thesis. If guidance is missed, disappointment-driven selling could follow.

Shareholder Returns

  • The quarterly dividend is being maintained at $0.35 per share.
  • The share repurchase authorization was raised to about $284 million, which is not small relative to the market cap.

Major Shareholders

In order: BlackRock Institutional Trust at about 13.6%, Fidelity at about 12.5%, Vanguard at about 6.6%, State Farm at about 6.0%, and the iShares Core S&P Small-Cap ETF at about 5.9%. The ownership structure is heavily weighted toward index and institutional money. South Korea's National Pension Service is confirmed not to hold the stock.

Risks to Watch

  • Business office furniture demand: Sensitive to remote and hybrid work and to corporate investment sentiment.
  • Tariffs: A cost variable significant enough that the company mentions tariff effects in its earnings commentary.
  • Integration risk: After a large acquisition, there is a possibility of higher costs and the loss of customers and dealers.
  • Residential building products (fireplaces, etc.): Affected by the housing market and interest rates.

Summary

HNI has grown larger through the acquisition, and the market appears to still be waiting to confirm that synergies are realized and demand recovers. That is one way to explain, as an estimate, why the stock is near where it was a year ago. What to watch in next quarter's results is whether volume growth actually turns positive and whether synergies are building up on schedule.
