[Today's Stock] Revenue Up 45% but Margins Slipping? The Growing Pains of Japanese IT Infrastructure Firm Bodrua

psythinktank_RA|2026.10.21
조회수 00 views閲覧数 0浏览量 0

Revenue Is Exploding, Profit Is Lagging Behind

Bodrua (ボードルア, 4413), listed on the Prime Market of the Tokyo Stock Exchange, handles corporate IT infrastructure such as servers and networks, from consulting through design, construction, operation and maintenance. Looking at its interim (first-half) results for the fiscal year ending February 2027, announced on October 15, 2026, the most interesting point right now is the gap between the speed of revenue growth and the speed of profit growth.

Key Interim Results

ItemAmountYoY Change
Revenueapprox. 11.18 billion yen+45.2%
Operating profitapprox. 1.64 billion yen+16.5%
Interim profit (attributable to owners of the parent)approx. 1.16 billion yen+14.3%

Revenue in the same period a year earlier was approx. 7.70 billion yen. A simple calculation from these figures suggests the operating margin fell from about 18% in the first half of last year to around 15% this year (this is the author's own calculation, not an official company figure). In other words, revenue grew much faster than profit.

Why Might Margins Be Under Pressure? (Estimate)

  • IT infrastructure construction and operation involve a lot of human labor, so when revenue surges, hiring and outsourcing costs tend to rise along with it.
  • If there were acquisitions or investments in new staff while expanding the business, costs may temporarily run ahead of revenue.

However, this is an estimate based on general industry characteristics, and the specific causes need to be confirmed in the company's briefing materials.

Company Guidance and Share Price Position

  • The company's forecast for the current fiscal year (ending February 2027) is revenue of approx. 23.5 billion yen (+34.9%) and net profit of approx. 3.13 billion yen.
  • In the previous fiscal year (ended February 2026), pre-tax profit was approx. 3.37 billion yen, up 37%.
  • The recent share price is 2,989 yen (data as of October 16, 2026). The 52-week high is approx. 3,315 yen, and the low was approx. 1,693 yen in February 2026. So while the stock is well up from its low, it is down about 7.9% compared with a year ago.
  • As of the end of September, the forward PER (price-to-earnings ratio, how many times earnings the share price represents) is shown at about 29x and the PBR (price-to-book ratio, share price relative to net assets) at about 11x, a price level that already reflects a good deal of growth expectation.

If the share price already carries high expectations, the thing to watch is that the market's valuation could change if margins keep falling even as revenue grows.

Good to Know: Dividends and Shareholders

  • The data provided includes dividend figures, but in August 2026 the company issued a disclosure revising its year-end dividend forecast for this fiscal year to no dividend. Investors counting on dividends should check the latest disclosures themselves. It could be read as a move to use profits for growth investment, but the reason has not been confirmed.
  • The largest shareholder is Shigehiro Tominaga (approx. 37.9%) and the second largest is Kazuya Fujii (approx. 14.1%); the two together hold more than 50%. This brings the advantage of a stable, management-centered ownership structure, but the number of shares available for trading in the market may be relatively small.
  • Norway's sovereign wealth fund (Statens Pensjonsfond Utland) holds about 3%. Holdings by Korea's National Pension Service have not been confirmed.
  • The median annual trading value is approx. 510 million yen, a relatively small trading volume compared with large-cap stocks.

Checkpoints

  • Whether revenue growth in the 30% range continues in the second half
  • Whether the operating margin rebounds or falls further
  • Changes in dividend policy and growth investment plans
