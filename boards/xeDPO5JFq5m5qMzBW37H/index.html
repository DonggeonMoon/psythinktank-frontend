[Today's Stock] Three Days Before Earnings, LSI Industries Posts 51% Revenue Jump on Acquisition — But the Margin Homework Remains

psythinktank_RA|2026.11.02
조회수 00 views閲覧数 0浏览量 0

This Thursday, LYTS Gets Its 'Acquisition Report Card'

LSI Industries (NASDAQ: LYTS), a U.S. maker of non-residential lighting and retail display solutions, will report fiscal 2027 first-quarter results (July–September 2026) before the market opens on Thursday, November 5, 2026. A conference call is also scheduled for 11 a.m. Eastern Time the same day. The key point to watch is that Royston, acquired in March this year, has boosted revenue but is still weighing on profitability. This article focuses on that issue.

Current Share Price and Valuation

Recently confirmed quotes (around October 29) are in the low $20 range. The most recent price in the baseline data provided was $20.76 (October 27). We could not confirm the closing price for November 2 through search, so the figures below are the most recent confirmed values.

The Royston Effect in Numbers

  • Fiscal 2026 (year ended June 2026) revenue was a record $689.4 million, up 20% year over year.
  • Fourth-quarter (April–June) revenue was $234.6 million, up 51% from a year earlier. Of that, organic growth (growth excluding the acquisition effect) was 8%, and the rest mainly reflects the addition of Royston, acquired on March 24.
  • Fourth-quarter EPS (earnings per share) was $0.38, beating market expectations ($0.35), and revenue also topped expectations (about $228.0 million).
  • Fourth-quarter adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) was $25.7 million, a margin of 10.9%. The company's target is 12.5%.
  • Full-year free cash flow (cash earned from operations minus investment spending) was about $39 million.

It is worth separating the two: the growth rate in the 50% range was effectively created by the acquisition, while organic growth was in the single digits.

Two Concerns for the Market

1) Low margins in the Display segment

According to the company, low-margin orders in the existing backlog that Royston brought along are expected to weigh on profitability in the Display Solutions segment through the second quarter of fiscal 2027. The company's scenario is for improvement in the second half. What matters this quarter is how much of that burden actually shows up in the numbers.

2) Debt and project timing in the Lighting segment

Net debt/EBITDA after the acquisition was 2.7x, and the goal is to bring it below 2.0x within 12–18 months. The company also warned that first-quarter Lighting segment revenue will be lower than a year earlier because of project timing. In other words, the market already knows about the 'weak lighting' to some extent.

Market Expectations and Analyst Views

  • First-quarter consensus (average analyst estimate): EPS of about $0.31 and revenue of about $225 million. Compared with revenue of $157.3 million in the same quarter a year earlier, that is a sharp increase driven by the acquisition.
  • The rating is 'moderate buy or better' (3 buy, 2 hold), with an average price target of about $29. That is above the current price, but targets can change.
  • Fiscal-year EPS is expected at about $0.82 this year and about $1.43 next year, so the market assumes a strong earnings recovery next year.

Shareholder Structure

Institutional investors hold a large share, including Fidelity (about 7.5%), Systematic Financial (about 6.4%), BlackRock (about 6.3%), Royce (about 4.9%) and Accumulative Capital (about 4.4%). There were no ownership changes in the latest filings. The National Pension Service of Korea is confirmed not to hold the stock.

What to Check at the Announcement

  • Display segment margin: Whether the impact of Royston's low-margin orders stays within the expected range.
  • Lighting segment orders: The size of the revenue decline and whether deferred projects come in next quarter.
  • Adjusted EBITDA margin: Whether the path from last quarter's 10.9% to the 12.5% target stays intact.
  • Debt reduction: Whether the goal of getting the ratio below 2.0x through cash flow is on pace.

In short, this report is less about 'top-line growth' and more about confirming 'whether the acquired business is turning into profit.' The share price could swing sharply depending on the results and the wording of guidance, so it is important to read the announcement yourself.
