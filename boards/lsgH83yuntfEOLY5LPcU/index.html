[Today's Stock] Half of Aircraft Engine Parts Profits Into Buybacks? Inside FTAI Aviation's $500 Million Bet

psythinktank_RA|2026.11.27
조회수 00 views閲覧数 0浏览量 0

FTAI Turns From a Leasing Company Into an 'Engine Parts Company'

FTAI Aviation (FTAI) buys and leases aircraft engines, and also dismantles used engines to resell them as parts. Its flagship is the CFM56 engine, widely used in narrow-body passenger jets around the world. Lately, the company's center of gravity has been shifting quickly from leasing to its 'Aerospace Products' segment.

Q3 Results: What the Numbers Say About the Shift

Key points from the 2026 third-quarter results announced on October 29:

  • Consolidated adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, adjusted by the company's own method): about $348.3 million, up 17% year over year
  • Aerospace Products segment: revenue of about $910 million (up 76% year over year), adjusted EBITDA of about $282.4 million (up 57%), margin of about 31%
  • Aviation Leasing segment: adjusted EBITDA of about $104 million. That is lower than the same period last year (about $134.4 million) but higher than the previous quarter.
  • Net debt: about $3.07 billion, or 2.2 times annualized EBITDA, below the target range set by management (2.5 to 3.0 times).

Some summaries report net income of about $149.1 million and diluted earnings per share (EPS) of $1.43, above market expectations (around $1.31). Figures differ slightly between data sources, so we recommend checking the exact values in the company's filings.

This Article's Theme: The $500 Million Accelerated Share Repurchase

Alongside earnings, the company said it is raising its buyback authorization to $1 billion, of which $500 million will be carried out as an accelerated share repurchase (ASR). In an ASR, the company pays a bank (here, RBC) up front and receives shares immediately, which are retired. The bank then buys the shares back in the market over time to settle. It is expected to be completed within the fourth quarter.

There are three reasons this decision is interesting.

  • Because leverage is below target, it can be read as a signal that the company has room to return capital to shareholders without adding debt.
  • At the same time, the company has been tidying up its funding structure, including issuing $1 billion of senior notes due 2034, securing a $2 billion secured financing facility, and redeeming high-coupon preferred shares.
  • However, there are reports that the stock swung sharply even right after the buyback announcement, suggesting the market did not treat it purely as good news.

Guidance: $2.3 Billion for 2027

ItemDetails
2026 consolidated adjusted EBITDA targetAbout $1.525 billion (Aerospace Products about $1.05 billion, Leasing about $475 million)
2027 consolidated adjusted EBITDA targetAbout $2.3 billion

The fact that the 2027 target is more than 50% higher than 2026 is at the heart of the debate over the current share price. The target relies on the assumption that strong growth in the engine parts business and orders in the new Power segment keep increasing. Reports say Power orders have exceeded 100. Note that some sources use a basis that excludes the Power segment, so check that the basis is the same when comparing.

Share Price: Extremely Volatile

According to the data provided, the recent share price as of November 23 was about $163.56, and the one-year change was -3.85%. In November there were several reports of days with moves of around 10% up or down, and some sources say the stock ranged from the $150s to the $190s. Closing prices differ by source, so we could not confirm the exact close on November 27; please check real-time quotes separately.

Wall Street views are also mixed. Morgan Stanley reportedly cut its price target from $360 to $310, while RBC raised its target from $250 to $265 and maintained an Outperform rating.

Shareholders and Dividends

Major shareholders include large institutions such as Capital International Investors (about 12.06%), Capital World Investors (about 11.11%), funds in the American Funds family, and BlackRock. South Korea's National Pension Service is not recorded as a holder. The annual dividend was given as $1.7, and the dividend yield at the current share price is around 1%.

Checkpoints

  • Whether Aerospace Products margins stay around 30%
  • Whether Leasing segment EBITDA continues its rebound
  • Whether the Power business, central to the $2.3 billion 2027 target, turns into actual revenue and profit
  • Additional buybacks and changes in leverage after the ASR is completed
  • CFM56 engine parts demand and supply chain conditions

In short, FTAI is a stock where high growth expectations come with large price volatility. The buyback shows management's confidence, but whether the targets are met will be settled by future results.
