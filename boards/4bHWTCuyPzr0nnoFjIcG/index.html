[Today's Stock] The Ticker Vanished? Why ASNOVA Swapped 9223 for 640A in Its Holding Company Shift

psythinktank_RA|2026.11.19
조회수 00 views閲覧数 0浏览量 0

First, a key point: 9223 no longer trades

Search for the Japanese scaffolding rental company ASNOVA (9223) and the ticker 9223 comes up first. But that code was delisted at the end of September 2026. The company used a sole share transfer to create a pure holding company, ASNOVA Companies (ticker 640A), and since October 1 this holding company has been listed and traded on the Tokyo Stock Exchange Growth market and the Nagoya Stock Exchange Next market. Existing shareholders received one holding company share for each share they held (1-for-1). So the stock investors should be looking at now is 640A.

Terms explained: A pure holding company does not run a business directly; it manages its subsidiaries. A share transfer is a method in which the existing company becomes a 100% subsidiary of a new parent company, and shareholders receive shares of the parent.

Why switch to a holding company?

  • The company said the holding structure would speed up decision-making at its subsidiaries and accelerate acquisitions and expansion in Southeast Asia.
  • ASNOVA's core business is renting wedge-type scaffolding to small and mid-sized contractors nationwide, alongside sales of new equipment, sales of used equipment, and scaffolding installation work.
  • The push to grow overseas operations (Vietnam, Singapore and elsewhere) appears to be behind this restructuring. This part is an interpretation based on company announcements and press reports.

Earnings: Sales are growing, but profit is still in the red

ItemDetails
Fiscal year ended March 2026Ordinary loss of -84 million yen (worse than the company's earlier forecast of -72 million yen)
Company outlook for fiscal year ending March 2027Targeting a return to profit with ordinary profit of 15 million yen
Q1 of fiscal year ending March 2027 (Apr–Jun)Consolidated sales up about 37% year on year, EBITDA (operating profit before depreciation) up about 20%. However, ordinary loss was -76 million yen

The Q1 ordinary loss is -76 million yen, while the full-year target is +15 million yen. That means a sharp improvement in profit is needed over the remaining quarters. The company is reported to have explained that part of the Vietnamese subsidiary's sales slipped into Q2 and that the full year is broadly on plan. Some analysts also point out that the equity ratio is low, in the low 20% range, and that interest-bearing debt is large, so the financial burden is worth watching.

Share price: A surge right after listing, then back to where it started

  • After the holding company listed, it hit a year-to-date high of 491 yen on October 2.
  • It then fell, reaching 358 yen (intraday) near the year's low on November 6. The most recent closing price I can confirm is 378 yen on November 10.
  • Based on the data provided, the latest share price is 364 yen, and the 1-year price change is -38.1%. The closing price for November 19 could not be confirmed on the web, so I won't state one.
  • The dividend is around 1 yen per year, so the dividend yield at 364 yen is only about 0.3%. The share price is more likely to hinge on whether earnings improve than on dividends.

Assuming roughly 12.3 million shares outstanding, the market capitalization is around 4.5 billion yen. This is an estimate back-calculated from previously reported figures.

Checkpoints

  • Confirm the ticker has changed to 640A, and keep in mind that materials based on the old code (9223) may be outdated
  • How much the Q1 loss of -76 million yen narrows in the Q2 (Jul–Sep) results
  • Whether the full-year return-to-profit outlook (ordinary profit of 15 million yen) is maintained
  • Whether overseas expansion and acquisitions actually translate into profit, and whether debt grows along the way
  • Liquidity risk from low trading volume and high major-shareholder ownership
