[Today's Stock] The Twist Behind ¥26 Billion Net Profit: Shinagawa Refractories (5351), Japan's Refractory Leader Yielding Over 5%

psythinktank_RA|2026.11.11
조회수 00 views閲覧数 0浏览量 0

Flashy numbers, yet profit is set to shrink this year? The two faces of Shinagawa Refractories

Shinagawa Refractories (品川リフラクトリーズ, Tokyo Stock Exchange Prime, 5351) makes refractories (bricks and cement-like materials that don't melt or collapse at high temperatures) used to line the inside of steel mills' blast furnaces and electric furnaces. They are replaced continually like consumables in the steelmaking process, so the company's fortunes move with the steel industry. The reason to look at this company now is a mixed set of numbers: "net profit jumped sharply last year, but is expected to fall this year."

1. Recent Share Price and Basic Information

  • Recent share price: about ¥1,847 (based on data provided as of November 6, 2026. The closing price for November 11 could not be confirmed)
  • In early October the stock traded in the ¥1,945–1,976 range, so it has slipped somewhat since.
  • Market capitalization: about ¥92.9 billion in early October; at the current share price it is estimated at roughly ¥87 billion (estimate).
  • The 1-year share price change was -0.22%, meaning the stock was essentially flat over the year.

2. Fiscal Year Ended March 2026: Both Sales and Profit Were Strong

What stands out is that net profit (¥26.0 billion) is far larger than ordinary profit (¥15.9 billion). One-time gains outside the core business may have lifted net profit (the specific breakdown could not be confirmed in this article, so this is an estimate). That makes it hard to read the 100%-plus growth in net profit at face value as underlying strength.

3. Fiscal Year Ending March 2027: Sales Up, Profit Down

Along with its first-quarter results in August 2026, the company raised its outlook for this year.

  • Sales forecast: ¥189.0 billion → ¥198.0 billion (about 4.8% increase)
  • Ordinary profit forecast: ¥13.0 billion → ¥14.0 billion (about 7.7% increase, but still below last year's ¥15.9 billion)
  • Net profit forecast: about ¥10.5 billion (a sharp drop from ¥26.0 billion last year)
  • First-half (April–September) forecast: sales ¥95.0 billion, operating profit ¥4.5 billion, net profit ¥6.5 billion

First-quarter (April–June) operating profit was reported at about ¥1.7 billion, down roughly 40% from a year earlier. The causes are reported to be widening losses in the engineering division, which designs and installs furnaces, and rising raw material and fuel costs. In other words, a structure in which "sales volume grows but the money left over shrinks" is this year's key challenge.

4. Dividend: Over 5% at the Current Share Price

The planned dividend this year is ¥95 (¥47 interim, ¥48 year-end), up ¥5 from last year. Calculated at ¥1,847, the dividend yield is about 5.1%. The expected payout ratio (the share of profit paid out as dividends) is in the low 40% range, suggesting a willingness to raise the dividend even in a year when profit declines. However, the dividend is a planned figure that can change at any time.

With largest shareholder JFE Steel holding more than one-third, the company has a stable customer and shareholder base, but results can be heavily influenced by a single steelmaker's capital spending and production plans, which is a risk factor. Korea's National Pension Service does not hold this stock. The median daily trading value is only about ¥170 million per year, so trading volume is thin and investors should watch for price swings when trading.

6. Points to Watch Ahead

  • Interim results announcement: Reportedly scheduled for around mid-November; the actual figures could not be confirmed. The key is whether the first-half outlook (¥4.5 billion operating profit) was achieved.
  • Whether profitability in the engineering division recovers
  • Raw material and fuel prices and the ability to pass costs on to product prices
  • Trends in Japan's steel output
  • Stable cash flow from the real estate and leisure segments (golf courses, hotels, etc.)

In sum, with Shinagawa Refractories, rather than last year's flashy net profit, you need to watch both "how far core profit recovers" and "whether the 5%-plus dividend is maintained."
