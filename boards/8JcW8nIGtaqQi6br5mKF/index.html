[Today's Stock] How the 'National Ballpoint Pen' Survived a Delisting Scare: Why Monami's Stock Rode a Rollercoaster

psythinktank_RA|2026.10.13
조회수 00 views閲覧数 0浏览量 0

A Beloved Stationery Company Facing the KRW 30 Billion Market Cap Wall

Monami (Monami Co., Ltd., KOSPI: 005360) is a stationery company founded in 1967. Besides writing and drawing instruments, best known for the 153 ballpoint pen, it also supplies ink cartridges and toner for Samsung Electronics printers and runs a cosmetics OEM/ODM business (manufacturing and developing products under another company's brand). This year, the key word for this stock is not earnings but delisting rules.

This article is based on prices and reports from around October 8, the most recent data I could verify. I could not confirm the October 12 closing price or market cap, so please check real-time quotes separately before trading.

How It Unfolded

DateWhat happened
July 1The market cap requirement for KOSPI-listed companies was raised to KRW 30 billion (to be raised further to KRW 50 billion in 2027)
June to early JulyMarket cap of about KRW 22.6 billion at end of June, below the threshold. Share price in the KRW 1,200 range
Mid-JulySurged on online sentiment that "we can't let the national ballpoint pen get delisted" and thematic buying. On July 14 it hit KRW 2,650 intraday (+29.90%), and reportedly reached KRW 3,445 on the 15th, up about 187% in two weeks
August 19 to October 1Market cap again fell below the threshold
October 2Designated as an administrative issue
October 7A court granted an injunction related to market-cap-based delisting, and Monami disclosed that the delisting process was suspended
October 8Share price rebounded, rising as much as 13% intraday and trading in the KRW 1,100 range

An administrative issue is a stock the exchange has separately designated for oversight because a delisting reason has arisen or may arise. Reports also said the exchange decided to temporarily suspend delisting procedures for stocks falling short on market cap.

Share Price: Back Where It Started After the Spike

As of October 8, the share price was around KRW 1,086, with the previous close reported at KRW 1,034. Multiplying by roughly 18.9 million shares outstanding gives a market cap of about KRW 19.5 billion. This is my own simple calculation; the market cap of about KRW 50.1 billion at the July 14 close has shrunk sharply in three months. The 1-year price change in the provided data is -48.91%.

In short, the July surge was a short-term phenomenon driven by rule changes and sentiment, not by a change in corporate value, and most of the gains have been reversed. For the same reason, the current rebound is merely a reaction to the fact that the legal process was paused, not a sign that the underlying problem has been solved.

Earnings Remain a Challenge

  • Consolidated operating loss: about KRW 2.7 billion in 2023, about KRW 3.8 billion in 2024, and about KRW 5.9 billion in 2025, widening each year
  • Consolidated net loss for 2025 was about KRW 10.6 billion, reportedly nearly double the previous year
  • Q1 this year: operating loss of about KRW 2.7 billion. Q2: revenue of about KRW 28.8 billion (-9.5% YoY) and operating loss of about KRW 1.3 billion; the loss narrowed somewhat but continues
  • More than 90% of revenue comes from stationery, a market that is shrinking as PCs and smartphones spread
  • Reportedly, the current ratio (liquid assets relative to debts due within a year) is below 100%, and short-term borrowings are about KRW 73.2 billion

The growth rate of 60.14% in the data can vary greatly depending on the basis, such as changes in the size of losses, so it is hard to conclude from this alone that the business has improved.

What Shareholders Should Note

  • Largest shareholder stake: Song Ha-kyung and 7 others hold about 28.19%, with almost no change in recent years. This suggests a stable ownership base, but also means a sizable float.
  • Dividends: At the March shareholders' meeting, a dividend of KRW 30 per share was approved. Because it was paid amid losses, some media outlets treated it as controversial. The company has paid dividends every year since 1997 but reportedly has no history of share buybacks or cancellations.
  • Restructuring: The merger with Monami Imaging Solution in November 2025 and the liquidation of its Shanghai subsidiary in March 2026 were reported.
  • National Pension Service: It holds no stake.

What to Watch Next

  • The merits ruling after the injunction and any follow-up action by the exchange: the suspension is temporary, so the outcome could change.
  • Whether market cap recovers stably to KRW 30 billion or more, or whether measures such as a capital increase or share cancellation to support the stock emerge
  • Whether the narrowing of losses continues in Q3 results and how the short-term borrowing burden is managed
  • With a median annual trading value of about KRW 130 million, which is small, the stock can swing sharply on a single headline

Summary

Monami has the strength of brand recognition, but its financial indicators and share price volatility call for a conservative view. The current share price is influenced more by regulations and sentiment than by business performance, so it is important to check rule changes, court and exchange decisions, and quarterly earnings together.
