[Today's Stock] A Car Parts Maker Giving Bitcoin to Shareholders and Buying a Brokerage? The Strange Transformation of Ikuyo

psythinktank_RA|2026.10.09
조회수 00 views閲覧数 0浏览量 0

Odd Moves for an Auto Parts Maker

Ikuyo (イクヨ, Tokyo Stock Exchange Standard 7273) is a Japanese company that makes automotive interior parts such as door trim covers and floor consoles, as well as exterior parts such as radiator grilles and bumpers. It also runs a small housing-related products business. Lately, though, news about the company is less about parts and more about M&A, a bitcoin gift for shareholders, and the acquisition of a digital asset brokerage. Today we look at what this unfamiliar transformation means for the share price and earnings.

P/E (price-to-earnings ratio) is the share price divided by earnings per share, and shows how expensive the stock is relative to profit. A figure near 300x means profit is very small, so the market is arguably pricing in future change rather than current earnings.

Three Prongs of the Transformation

1. Growing Sales Through M&A

According to the fiscal year ended March 2026 results, the addition of two consolidated subsidiaries lifted sales to about 30.1 billion yen and profit increased. However, net income includes extraordinary gains such as the sale-and-leaseback (selling an asset and leasing it back) of the Atsugi plant and penalty income related to a share acquisition. So it is hard to say the core business improved by that much. Sales for the next fiscal year (ending March 2027) are forecast at about 34.4 billion yen.

2. Acquiring a Digital Asset Brokerage

In May 2026 it was reported that the company would make Digital Asset Securities, a financial instruments business operator, a subsidiary. It is a notable decision for an auto parts maker to step into financial services. There are not yet enough confirmable figures to say how much this business will contribute to earnings.

3. Bitcoin Gift for Shareholders

A program is known in which bitcoin worth a total of 10 million yen is given by lottery to shareholders who held 500 or more shares as of March 31, 2026 and continued to hold them through June 30. When it was announced in late May, the share price was reported to have turned upward. A headline also says the stock surged in late trading on September 9 on news of introducing an ongoing annual shareholder benefit program, but with no article body available, I could not confirm details such as the size of the rise or the conditions.

Who Owns the Company

  • Nitto (Nitto KK) 42.59%
  • SUNRISE ORIENTAL CAPITAL 17.7%
  • Hiroshi Mizuno 2.33%, Hironari Sakai 2.14%, Yaizu Body Kogyo 1.94%

The top two shareholders together hold around 60%, so the free float is small. Large shareholding reports, which disclose changes in ownership, were filed several times in 2025–2026, but I found no basis to say that a tender offer (TOB) or similar event took place. Still, a large major-shareholder stake means trading can be thin and that major shareholders' decisions heavily influence the company's direction. Median annual trading value is small at about 60 million yen, so prices can move sharply when investors buy or sell.

What to Watch

  • Whether the quarterly results announced in mid-August show profit contributions from the acquired subsidiaries
  • Whether core profit is growing even after excluding extraordinary gains
  • The concrete sales scale of the digital asset brokerage business
  • Whether the shareholder benefit program is a one-off event or an ongoing policy
  • The dividend is 3 yen per share, so the shareholder return effect is not large

In short, Ikuyo started as a traditional manufacturer and is rapidly broadening its business portfolio. The pace of change is fast, but as the P/E suggests, profit has not yet caught up, and the share price is lower than a year ago. Whether the transformation is proven in the numbers is the most important thing to watch.
