[Today's Stock] Profits Up Double Digits Every Year, Yet the Stock Is Down 30% in a Year: Japan's Drugstore Chain Genky

psythinktank_RA|2026.10.14
조회수 00 views閲覧数 0浏览量 0

Sales and Profits Are Growing, So Why Has the Stock Lagged?

Genky DrugStores (9267), listed on the Prime Market of the Tokyo Stock Exchange, is a drugstore chain that started in Fukui Prefecture, Japan. Besides medicine and cosmetics, it sells food and household goods at low prices under an 'EDLP (Everyday Low Price, a approach that keeps prices low at all times without relying on sales promotions)' strategy. Earnings keep growing, yet the share price is well below where it was a year ago. This gap is the most interesting point about the stock right now.

For reference, the share price below is as of October 9 at 3,455 yen, and I could not confirm the quote for October 14 through search. Quote pages from the end of September show the mid-3,400 yen range.

It is natural to read the low dividend yield as a sign that this is a growth company that puts profits into opening new stores rather than paying dividends. This is an estimate.

FY June 2026 Results: Double-Digit Growth

  • Net sales of about 220.6 billion yen, up 9.9% year on year
  • Operating profit of about 10.985 billion yen, up 13.7%
  • Ordinary profit of about 11.157 billion yen, up 12.7%
  • Net profit of about 7.875 billion yen, up 11.4%

The company explains that price competitiveness in fresh food was a key factor. Drugstores with a high share of food see frequent customer visits, so sales tend to be stable.

What Seems to Have Weighed on the Stock

Guidance for the next fiscal year (FY June 2027) is net sales of about 244.0 billion yen (up 10.6%), operating profit of about 12.0 billion yen (up 9.2%), and ordinary profit of about 12.1 billion yen. According to media reports, net profit growth was only 4.1%, below the market consensus average. The slowdown of operating profit growth to single digits may have weighed on the stock. However, the exact reason for the decline cannot be determined, and this explanation is an estimate.

The industry environment is also a variable. Competition among Japanese drugstores is fierce and price competition in food continues, so in a high-volume, low-margin model, rising labor and logistics costs can erode profit margins.

The Key to Growth: Room to Open Stores

According to the company's strategy materials from January this year, the store count plan is 538 for FY June 2026, 638 for FY June 2027, and 751 for FY June 2028. The company also said it currently operates in only five prefectures, so there are many areas left to enter. If the plan proceeds as laid out, top-line growth will continue, but the faster new stores increase, the heavier the initial costs and operating burden become. Whether the plan is achieved needs to be checked in future earnings releases.

Shareholder Composition

  • Fujinaga International Capitals: 37.33% (largest shareholder)
  • Fidelity Management & Research: 6.69%
  • Fidelity Low-Priced Stock Fund: 5.61%
  • Orbis Investment Management: about 5%
  • ELLEROSE: 4.51%

Because the largest shareholder holds a large stake, the number of shares available for trading is relatively small. On the other hand, overseas asset managers such as Fidelity and Orbis hold more than 5%, which shows interest from foreign institutions. Korea's National Pension Service does not hold the stock. It is also worth knowing that stocks with a small free float can see larger price swings when trading volume is low.

What to Watch Next

  • Whether operating profit growth in the next quarter comes in line with guidance
  • Whether sales at existing stores are growing (underlying strength excluding the effect of new openings)
  • The pace of openings and profitability in newly entered regions
  • The impact of food price competition and rising costs on profit margins

In summary, Genky is a company whose profits have grown at double-digit rates, but concerns about a slowing growth pace appear to be priced into the stock. Whether those concerns are excessive will be decided by upcoming quarterly results.
