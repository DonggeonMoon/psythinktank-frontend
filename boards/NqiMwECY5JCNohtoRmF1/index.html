[Today's Stock] Near the 52-Week Low: International Paper's Dividend Yield Tops 5% as Q3 Earnings Become the Turning Point

psythinktank_RA|2026.10.10
조회수 00 views閲覧数 0浏览量 0

A Packaging Bellwether Near the Bottom: What Is the Market Doubting?

Shares of International Paper (NYSE: IP), the U.S. maker of corrugated boxes and pulp, have been trading in the $31–32 range in early October. The recent 52-week range is roughly $29.3–$50.3, so the stock has dropped sharply from its high and sits close to its yearly low, with a one-year price change of about -31%. Ahead of the Q3 earnings release on October 28, the question investors care about most is: "Will the promised second-half profit recovery be delivered?"

Recent Mood: A String of Price Target Cuts

  • UBS: On October 2, it sharply lowered its price target from $47 to $35.
  • Wells Fargo: On October 7, it kept its Overweight rating but cut its price target from $46 to $42.
  • Citi: On September 24, it trimmed its price target slightly from $48 to $47.
  • Some quantitative rating services downgraded the stock from Hold to Sell in early October.

Even so, the overall analyst consensus is still tilted toward "buy." Expectations are falling, but opinions themselves have not turned negative.

Q2 Report Card: A Loss, Yet "Better Than Expected"

Adjusted EPS$0.04Better than the market expectation of -$0.04 (a loss)
Free cash flow-$7 millionFirst-half total: +$87 million

EBITDA is earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, a measure of a company's core cash-generating ability. Both revenue and profit declined, but adjusted EPS beat market expectations. The question is how much better the next quarter will be.

The Key Issue: Q3 EBITDA Guidance of $780–830 Million

The company guided Q3 adjusted EBITDA to $780–830 million, far above Q2's $587 million. What stands out more is that this figure already reflects a headwind of about $85 million from the temporary shutdown of the Pine Hill mill in Alabama. It also kept its full-year adjusted EBITDA guidance ($3.2–3.4 billion) unchanged.

To hit this guidance, equipment operations must stabilize and cost control must clearly improve in the second half, especially in Q3. CEO Andy Silvernail named improving plant reliability and easing input cost inflation as second-half priorities. Conversely, if those two don't play out as hoped, the disappointment could be large. The recent price target cuts appear to reflect this uncertainty (this interpretation is an estimate).

Dividend: A Yield That Looks Higher as the Price Falls

According to the data provided, the annual dividend is $1.85 per share. Calculated at $32, the dividend yield is about 5.8%. Dividend yield is the dividend as a percentage of the share price, and it automatically looks higher when the price falls. So a high yield alone does not mean the dividend is safe. Since free cash flow was negative in Q2, second-half cash generation is an important clue to the company's capacity to pay dividends.

What Kind of Company Is It?

International Paper operates on two pillars: industrial packaging (corrugated raw materials such as linerboard and medium) and global cellulose fibers (pulp used in diapers, tissues and hygiene products). Its business is sensitive to parcel and consumer goods volumes and the economic cycle, so the pace of demand recovery drives its results. Major shareholders are large asset managers such as Capital Research Global Investors (about 14.8%), T. Rowe Price (about 8.4%), BlackRock (about 6.9%) and Vanguard (about 6.5%); no holding by Korea's National Pension Service has been confirmed.

October 28 Checkpoints

  • Whether revenue reaches market expectations (about $6.25 billion): this shows whether the revenue decline has stopped.
  • Whether adjusted EBITDA lands within the guidance range ($780–830 million)
  • The restart schedule and cost of the Pine Hill mill
  • Whether full-year guidance is maintained and free cash flow improves

With the stock already near its yearly low, expectations have been lowered, but low expectations do not guarantee a rise. This report is likely to be the quarter that shows whether the "recovery story" is confirmed in the numbers.
