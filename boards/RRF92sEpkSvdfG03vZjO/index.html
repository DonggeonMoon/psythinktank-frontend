[Today's Stock] Record Earnings, Yet the Share Price Is Near Half Its Peak: What Happened to Dalba Global?

psythinktank_RA|2026.11.01
조회수 00 views閲覧数 0浏览量 0

Earnings Are Rising, So Why Is the Stock Falling?

Dalba Global (483650), which runs the vegan cosmetics brand d'Alba, listed on the KOSPI in 2025 and has grown its sales quickly since. Lately, though, its share price has moved in the opposite direction of its earnings growth. This article looks at where that gap came from.

Where It Stands in Numbers

  • Recent share price: About 148,000 won as of October 26 (based on the data provided). Late-October prices appear to have hovered around 150,000 won. November 1 is a market holiday (Sunday), so the most recent trading-day close should be used as the reference; closing prices for October 30 and 31 could not be confirmed.
  • 52-week range: About 116,000 won to 283,000 won. The current price is well below the high.
  • Since mid-August: The stock climbed to around 246,000 won on August 11, then fell to around 160,000 won in mid-September and about 150,000 won in late October.
  • 1-year price change: About -1.1%. The price is similar to a year ago, but it swung widely in between.

Earnings Are Clearly Growing Fast

In the third quarter of 2025, sales were 1,173億 won (117.3 billion won) and operating profit was 167億 won (16.7 billion won). In the second quarter of this year, sales were 1,869億 won (186.9 billion won) with an operating margin of 25.3%. The operating margin is the share of sales that remains as operating profit, showing how much the company keeps from its core business.

For the third quarter, the company is reported to have guided for sales of about 1,700億 won (170 billion won) and an operating margin of 17%. That is lower than the second quarter, but still a big increase from a year earlier. Brokerage estimates for operating profit are generally in the range of 320億 to 340億 won (32 to 34 billion won). These are estimates, made before the confirmed results were released.

What Is Weighing on the Stock

1. Expectations reset after Q2

Because second-quarter results were so strong, market expectations ran high, and the selling that followed the announcement is read as profit-taking. The fact that the third-quarter margin guidance is lower than the second quarter's also added to the pressure.

2. A stronger won

Dalba Global gets a large share of its sales from overseas. When the won strengthens, money earned abroad is worth less when converted back into won, so reported sales come out lower. One brokerage thought this could leave third-quarter sales about 6% below market expectations. On the other hand, some analysts say the hit to profit is relatively small, because overseas costs are also paid in local currencies.

3. High volatility

As a growth stock with a short track record since listing, its price swings sharply on hopes and disappointments. The 52-week high is more than double the low, which shows this.

Notable Points in the Shareholder Structure

  • Largest shareholder Ban Seong-yeon and 6 others: holding steady at 21.15%
  • National Pension Service: down from 9.5% as of September 21 to 8.48% as of October 31

The National Pension Service's stake fell by roughly 1 percentage point. The filings alone don't tell us why, so we can't draw a conclusion, but it is worth noting that a large institution's holding changed during a period of falling prices. Meanwhile, as of September, FIL LIMITED (a Fidelity affiliate) and 16 others held 5.08%.

What to Watch Next

  • Confirmed Q3 results: Whether sales land within the brokerage estimate range, and whether operating profit exceeds the 320億 won level
  • Sales by region: The share of overseas sales, including the U.S., and how fast they are growing
  • Exchange rate: How a continued strong won affects sales and margins
  • Supply and demand: Changes in the stakes of major shareholders such as the National Pension Service

Brokerages generally set target prices around 300,000 won (NH Investment & Securities at 300,000 won, Daol Investment & Securities at 310,000 won), far above the current share price. However, a target price is only a forecast, not a guaranteed figure, and it can be cut if earnings fall short of expectations.

Summary

Even though its profit is growing fast, Dalba Global's share price has come well down from its peak, as higher expectations, a strong won and concerns about slowing margins combined. The key variable ahead is whether the confirmed third-quarter results show real earnings strength.
