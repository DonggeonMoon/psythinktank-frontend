[Today's Stock] Core Sales Were Strong, So Why Did the Stock Slump? Dick's Sporting Goods and the Foot Locker Bill

psythinktank_RA|2026.10.03
조회수 00 views閲覧数 0浏览量 0

Key Summary: The Core Business Is Growing, but the Foot Locker Acquisition Is Dragging

Dick's Sporting Goods (NYSE: DKS), the largest sporting goods retailer in the U.S., was trading in the $133 range during the session on October 2, 2026 (previous close: $132.85). According to the data, its one-year price change is about -42%, putting it near its 52-week low. At the center of this decline is the sharp cut to the Foot Locker segment's guidance (the earnings forecast a company provides) that came with the second-quarter results in late August.

Q2 Results: The Core Business Was Fine

  • Second-quarter net income was $315 million ($3.50 per share), down from $381 million ($4.71 per share) in the same period a year earlier. Adjusted earnings per share were $3.53.
  • Comparable store sales (the sales growth rate counting only existing stores) at the DICK'S segment rose 4.9%. The company explained that both average transaction value and transaction count increased, helped by the 2026 FIFA World Cup.
  • Foot Locker's comparable store sales, on the other hand, fell 3.6%.

Right after the results, the stock plunged more than 20% in pre-market trading, amid assessments that the results fell short of market expectations.

How Much the Guidance Changed

ItemPrevious ForecastRevised Forecast
Company-wide full-year adjusted EPS$13.50–$14.50$11–$12
Foot Locker operating profit/loss$110–$150 million profit$40–$80 million loss
Foot Locker full-year comparable store sales-Flat to -2%

The company explained that discount promotions intensified in the sneaker and apparel markets as the quarter progressed, and that Foot Locker was hit harder because of its heavy reliance on older designs and on new-release and retro model launches. In other words, this problem has more to do with the nature of the acquired business than with Dick's own competitiveness. Still, the fact that post-acquisition integration is not moving as fast as expected is a burden.

Market Reaction: Lowered Expectations and Insider Buying

  • On October 1, Truist cut its price target from $135 to $128.
  • The consensus of 17 analysts is still 'Buy,' with an average price target of about $174. However, this target may include opinions issued before the earnings downgrade, so it could fall further.
  • Insider buying has continued. Director William Colombo bought 913 shares on September 1, and director Larry Fitzgerald bought 1,860 shares (about $245,000) at $131.67 per share on September 23. The chief financial officer (CFO) also reportedly bought 7,707 shares (about $1 million) at $129.75 per share in late September, according to filings.

Insider buying does not guarantee a rebound in the stock price. However, it can be read as a signal that management considers the current price low.

A Risk to Watch: Class Action Lawsuit

A securities class action has been filed on behalf of investors who acquired the stock between September 8, 2025 and August 24, 2026, and the deadline to apply for lead plaintiff is November 3, 2026. The lawsuit is at the allegation stage, and no outcome has been determined. Still, the litigation process can bring costs and uncertainty.

Who Holds the Stock

Based on recent filings, major institutional holders include Wellington Management at about 11.7%, BlackRock at about 8.6%, Fidelity at about 5.9%, Managed Account Advisors at about 5.6%, and Vanguard at about 5.0%. Korea's National Pension Service is confirmed not to hold the stock.

Points to Watch Ahead

  • Size of Foot Locker's loss: We need to see whether it stays within the guided loss range of $40–$80 million.
  • Discount competition in footwear: Inventory levels and promotional intensity at major brands such as Nike will shape margins.
  • Strength of the core business: The key is whether the DICK'S segment's comparable store sales can keep growing after the World Cup.
  • Lawsuit progress: The steps after the November 3 deadline need to be checked.

Wrap-Up

Dick's is a rare case in which one acquired business sharply cut the full-year profit outlook while the core business keeps growing. With the stock more than 40% lower than a year ago, one could argue the market has already priced in considerable concern. But if Foot Locker fails to recover, expectations could fall even further. The key question is which lasts longer: the growth of the core business or the losses of the acquired one.
