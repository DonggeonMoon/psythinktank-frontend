[Today's Stock] Kodama Chemical's Sales Grew 5x After a Big Acquisition — This Year, Without the One-Off Gain, Is the Real Test

Kodama Chemical Industry (児玉化学工業, 4222.T): A Japanese Auto and Housing Parts Maker That Grew Through M&A

Kodama Chemical Industry is a Japanese plastic molded parts maker listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange (TSE). Its size changed dramatically in April 2025, when it made Mepro Holdings, a metalworking auto parts maker, a wholly owned subsidiary. This article outlines where the company stands today, based on PSY Thinktank data and publicly available earnings and news.

1. Business Overview

The company has traditionally made auto parts and housing parts based on resin (plastic) molding. Its current business breaks down roughly as follows.

  • Auto parts (Mobility): Interior and exterior parts such as instrument panels, bumpers, door panels and trims, luggage trims, pillar garnishes, side mudguards and consoles, as well as cylinder heads.
  • Housing and appliance parts (Living Space): Bathroom mirror cabinets, bathroom ceilings, bathtub front panels (aprons), washing machine fans, drain traps, kitchen and sanitary parts, and home appliance parts.
  • Other: Entertainment-related products and rice transplanter parts.

On April 1, 2025, the company completed its acquisition of Mepro Holdings. Mepro makes auto parts using three metalworking methods: aluminum die casting (injecting molten metal into molds), powder metallurgy, and iron forging. With the acquisition, Kodama Chemical became an auto parts group handling metal parts in addition to plastic parts.

2. Growth Points

① Acquisition pushes sales up more than fivefold

Consolidating Mepro roughly quintupled sales from the prior year, and operating profit also rose sharply. PSY Thinktank's growth rate of 343.9% appears to reflect this rapid expansion in scale. However, we were not able to confirm the specific calculation basis for PSY Thinktank's growth rate in this article.

② What's behind the ¥23.5 billion net income: one-off "negative goodwill"

Net income for the fiscal year ended March 2026 came to about 9 times operating profit because of a gain on negative goodwill (負ののれん発生益) of about ¥22 billion. Negative goodwill is the gain that arises when the acquisition price is lower than the acquired company's net assets. It is an accounting gain recognized only once, so it is hard to view it as cash-generating ability or recurring earnings. This one-off gain disappearing is also why this year's net income is forecast to fall 94.1%.

③ Solid progress in Q1 this year

Ordinary profit for Q1 (April–June) of the fiscal year ending March 2027, announced on August 12, 2026, was ¥682 million. That is about 40.1% of the company's full-year forecast of ¥1.7 billion. Reaching 40% of the annual target in the first quarter alone is a positive sign. That said, quarterly profits can swing due to seasonality or one-off factors, so it's worth checking whether this trend continues in the first-half results scheduled for mid-November.

④ Synergies from combining plastic and metal parts

The company cited management stability through greater scale and a stronger portfolio, as well as technical and functional synergies, as the purposes of the acquisition. Being able to offer resin interior/exterior parts together with metal powertrain and structural parts could help broaden its customer base. However, whether these synergies actually show up in orders and improved margins should still be considered unproven.

  • Private equity (PE) is the largest shareholder: Japanese PE firm Endeavour United holds 31.57%. It invested in Kodama Chemical when the company applied for turnaround ADR (a process for restructuring finances through agreement with creditors outside of court) in January 2020, and the turnaround process ended in June 2023.
  • Related-party transaction: Mepro Holdings was originally owned by a fund under Endeavour United. In effect, the listed company bought another company that its largest shareholder had owned.
  • Possible stake sale (exit): According to an October 2023 Mergermarket report, Endeavour United was seeking to sell its stake in Kodama Chemical and had sent information materials to potential buyers. The same report said the fund then held a 66.71% economic interest, including class shares (Class A shares), and 33.68% of voting rights. We did not separately confirm whether the class shares remain outstanding or how the sale is progressing.
  • Institutional flows: Korea's National Pension Service does not hold the stock. Median trading value is about ¥220 million, placing it among small caps with more limited liquidity than large caps.

4. Risk Factors

  • One-off gain illusion: Most of last year's ¥23.5 billion net income came from negative goodwill. Calculating the PER (price-to-earnings ratio) on last year's net income can make the stock look far cheaper than it really is, so it should be assessed against this year's forecast net income of ¥1.4 billion.
  • Profit decline expected this year: The company itself forecasts sales of -3.3% and operating profit of -17.9%. Its plan calls for lower profits even on a core business basis.
  • Dependence on the auto industry: With most sales coming from auto parts, results are sensitive to vehicle production volumes, the trade environment such as U.S. tariffs, and raw material (resin, aluminum, iron) prices.
  • Integration (PMI) risk: Because it acquired a company several times its own size, unexpected costs could arise in integrating organization, production and finances.
  • Overhang from the largest shareholder's stake sale: Because a PE firm aims to recover its investment, a large stake sale or conversion of class shares into common shares could weigh on the share price and supply/demand. Conversely, a tender offer could emerge during a sale of management control, but this is merely a scenario, not a confirmed fact.
  • Financial history and liquidity: The company has previously gone through turnaround ADR. Also, because trading value is not large, price impact (slippage) when trading can be significant.
  • Currency: For investors outside Japan, fluctuations in the yen exchange rate also affect returns.

5. Overall View

Kodama Chemical Industry was once a company going through a turnaround process, but by acquiring Mepro Holdings, previously owned by its PE largest shareholder, it has transformed into an auto parts group with sales in the ¥80 billion range. It is true that sales and operating profit rose sharply. However, most of last year's net income was a one-off accounting gain, and the company's plan for this year calls for lower profits.

So when looking at this stock, it's better to check the following three things rather than simply noting that the numbers got bigger.

  • Whether Q1's solid progress (40.1% of ordinary profit forecast) continues in the first-half results in mid-November
  • Whether synergies from integrating plastic and metal parts show up in orders and margins
  • How and when the largest shareholder, Endeavour United, unwinds its stake

The share price is down -5.25% over the past year, suggesting it has not yet substantially priced in the top-line growth from the acquisition. Whether this is an opportunity or a fair discount reflecting the one-off gain and the overhang from the largest shareholder's sale should be judged by future earnings and changes in the shareholder base.
